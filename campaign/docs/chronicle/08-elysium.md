## The Expert

Olly drove Rami to the chantry in his old grey Civic.

Rami didn't talk. His left heel was up on its toes and his knee going, a fast, nervous shake, the whole way, and one finger
tapping the window at half that tempo, and he stared out into the middle distance at nothing.

Olly took comfort from him anyway. This was going to go better than usual. Rami was going to be there. Finally, they were going
to get some respect.

They got there half an hour early, because Rami would have been insufferable if he'd thought he was being made late, and they
waited out of sight across the street, and nobody saw them. While they waited, two people came out of the chantry and left. Then
Ethan's car pulled up, a Camry, a nice trim, the kind of car that blends in anywhere, with Ethan and Ralph and Rose in it.

"Where have you been?" said Olly. "He's been waiting half an hour." He lowered his voice. "Rami's the expert here. We should
probably let him lead."

Rami gave him a look out of the side of his eyes. "I'm not here to grovel. I'm here because there was a request for assistance."

"Definitely don't want to grovel."

"You start grovelling," somebody said, "that's all you'll ever do."

Rami looked closely at Ethan, and then at Ralph, for longer than was comfortable.

"The most important thing to do," he said, "with these long nights, is to carve out a little space for yourself."

It was meant as advice. Both of them got that. It came out as something between a warning and a pat on the head.

---

Dominik met them near the door this time, and walked up with them.

"Welcome. I was just taking care of some other matters. It'll take me a moment to get my things prepared. Please, come in, make
yourselves at home. Do you need anything to feed on?"

Ralph said he would pass. Ethan, a little reluctantly, passed too; Ralph had already said no, and he was being careful not to take
too much. Ralph understood the offer, or thought he did. It was the Camarilla's manners. Dominik had asked for this meeting, which
kept them from going out to hunt, and so, as the host, he would make sure there was something on offer. Whether that meant bagged
blood or somebody waiting in the wings for the purpose, Ralph didn't know.

They were in the sitting room: couches, a futon, things on display, a few books on shelves, more for show than for use. Beyond it the
chantry went on into labs and studies and libraries. It was Rami's first time here. He declined to sit. He walked the room slowly with
his hands clasped behind his back, so they couldn't touch anything by accident, and studied every object on every wall and shelf.

Olly went the other way round, counterclockwise to Rami's clockwise, poking around. Not looking for anything, exactly. Seeing what opened.

There was a glass bowl, with a sort of wavy ripple in the glass, and keys in it. A car key with a house key on the same ring. A ring of
eight or so little keys, the kind that open fire safes. And an old key, a brass one, a hundred years old or more, with big fixed teeth and
a fleur-de-lis on it.

He palmed the old one. It was in his pocket before he'd finished thinking about it.

*Oh, man*, he thought. *I'm going to get in trouble for this.*

---

Ethan watched people, not things.

Past the end of the sitting room there were three short steps, and a wide hallway going off to several rooms, and at the far end of it a huge
heavy wooden desk, with a giant painting behind it hung over what had to be a window, probably boarded up behind. Dominik was down there,
moving chairs, clearing things off the desk, getting it ready for them.

He was humming. Absent-mindedly, to himself. Ethan caught pieces of the melody, and he thought he'd heard it before somewhere, and he tried to
catch enough of it to hold on to. It stayed just at the edge of his mind. Then Dominik moved out of view and out of earshot, and it was gone.

Rami had stopped in front of a foot.

The bones of one, reconstructed. A foot has a great many small bones in it, and this was like one of those dinosaur exhibits where some of the
bones are real and the rest are careful replicas filling out the shape: you could see which were which. A human foot. There was a tiny
inscription plate beside it. Rami spent a long time there before he moved on.

Ralph, bored, looked for anything to read. There were low bookshelves all round the room, and he took them for the library's rejects, because
there was obviously a real library somewhere else. Rejects or not, they were interesting. A lot of them were popular novels with some fun or
weird take on vampires, and he thought at first that someone was making a joke. Then he saw how they were shelved.

On the left, books where no part was accurate. Toward the middle, a mixed bag. And as they went right, he recognized a few titles in each section,
and he realized that the ones at the far right edged into Masquerade-breach territory. A couple of those at the very end he'd never heard of. He
picked them up and flipped through, and either the author had been Kindred or somebody's ghoul had written them. Either way, you could see a head
rolling for it, out there in the world.

He made a note of the titles, for later.

## The Lecture

"So wonderful," said Dominik, returning. "You are Rami, I presume."

He brought them all into his office, where the body was not on display, and sat, and had Rami sit directly in front of him and to his right, and the
rest of them fan out behind.

"So. Tell me what you know about blood alchemy."

Rami looked at him for a moment, hesitant. Then he began, and once he had begun it snowballed. The rest of them sat through an apt pupil and an
enraptured teacher for about an hour and a half.

Ethan knew how to sit still, look into the middle distance, and think about nothing at all. The lights were on. Nobody was home.

Ralph did his best to look interested. It was all over his head, but he wanted to make a good impression.

Olly tried to pay attention to the parts he didn't already know, and not to touch the key in his pocket, and not to look like someone not touching a
key in his pocket.

And then he was horrified.

Rami was talking clinically. Matter-of-factly. There were so many ways this could go terribly wrong for someone like Olly, and Rami listed them, and
listed the precautions against them, and Olly recognized the precautions. Things Rami had done to him that he had never realized were precautions at
all. Without them he could have melted from the inside. His whole throat could have burned out. It went on, list after list, ways to maim or kill
someone like him permanently, and some of it sounded to Olly like the voice of experience.

Dominik was fascinated.

Rose leaned over to Ethan, not hiding that she was speaking, just keeping it quiet. "Were you aware of much of this?"

He wasn't.

"This one's a gold mine of information. I think we should take him to meet Evelyn. If he'll let us." She glanced at Dominik. "If Dominik here doesn't end
up recruiting him somehow."

*That sounds like a big deal*, Ethan thought. He took it in. "I'll see what I can do to make it happen."

Eventually Dominik nudged the conversation on. He felt, it seemed, that he had the rudiments now, and maybe a little extra. "So. It appears we have found
the body of one such athanor corporis. What sorts of things can one make with them?"

He offered a couple of examples of things he had seen. Rami gave a shorter spiel this time, which was still a spiel, and all of them came out of it knowing
more than they had. Things that could turn you into a bat. Things that let you pass through walls, or sink into the earth. Something that let you travel by
moonlight, under certain conditions, to places much farther away. Something that let you breathe fire. Some of it they'd heard of. Some of it was new to all
of them.

Olly listened to the catalogue and thought: he didn't know which of these Rami had been brewing in him. Rami had never told him. At this point it seemed
pretty clear why. He was an expensive, valuable asset, and Rami didn't want him running his mouth, or trying to make money off it.

---

"I believe," said Dominik, "that Rami and I will continue our engagement here." He looked at the rest of them. "The rest of you have proven profitable once
again. I would like to see about securing you as assets more formally. What are you doing Tuesday next?"

"Nothing on my calendar," said Ethan.

"It is an Elysium. We will be meeting at the University. There is a ballroom; we will be hosting. It is neutral territory, so if—" a gesture to Rose "—Evelyn
wishes to appear, or—" a gesture to all of them "—if some of your uncouth companions wish to join, they may, so long as all understand that it is a safe
space, where none may harm another, under the protection of Prince Lucian. I would wish for you to be there. It is a good opportunity for you to engage in the
proper breadth of Kindred society."

Under that, audibly: *you have learned not to pee in the house. I will let you in the house a little.*

He dismissed them. Rami he kept back. Rami nodded to Olly: "Leave the car here, I'll bring it back. You can get a ride?"

"No problem."

On the way out Ethan stopped at the foot, since Rami had. The plate beside it had a title, *The Foot of the Methuselah*, and a quotation in another language that had a Latin
feel to it and definitely wasn't Latin.

Olly looked for a keyhole on the way out, idly, without making a scene. There were three or four people in the halls; ghouls, he assumed, mostly. Nothing looked
like it would take the key.

It would keep. It sounded like they'd be back.

## Davos for Vampires

"So, Rose," said Ethan, driving. "What's an Elysium?"

"Roman heaven," Olly offered. "I think."

"Greek, I think."

"Why not both."

Rose's version was that it was Davos for vampires. The World Economic Forum, only more local. From where she sat, mostly a cock-measuring contest. The Prince
gathered people around him. The Anarch Barons were technically allowed to come, and usually made a perfunctory appearance unless there was a real dispute that
needed mediating: *Yes, I'm still here. No, I don't cede territory. Yes, we'll keep the fighting on the streets. See you later.* But it was a phenomenal way to
network. And you got a real sense of how many different views there were, even among people ostensibly on the same side.

"What should my priority be, while I'm there?" said Ethan.

"Right now? Don't make enemies. I don't have a clear sense yet of which relationships would be most valuable to us. But people who know things about the lake. If
we can find anything out along those lines."

"About the what?"

"The lake. Ontario."

"Okay," said Olly, who had heard of it. "And what's the situation with thin-bloods at Elysium?"

"Same as everyone. It's a time of peace. And you're invited, by Dominik, so you have cover. No one will kill you on sight. Even if there's cause, they'll have to
sort it out after."

"Well, that's good to know. But if your name starts getting around, isn't that bad for us? How is networking not bad for us? Aren't they basically trying to kill
thin-bloods?"

Rose didn't quite say it. But Olly heard what she meant: *you're different. You've proven yourselves. You've got people backing you. The normal rules don't apply to
you.*

"Not as long as we're useful," said Ralph. "Listen. Olly. How long do you want to live?"

"We're well past that point."

"I don't know about you. I plan on being here forever."

He'd been thinking about the books on the right-hand end of the shelf. One of them had been about diablerie, and he'd understood more of it than he'd let on. Drain
another one of them to death, and you took in some of what they were. You might get their powers. You might get some of their memories. And if you were particularly
unlucky, they might overpower you from the inside and take you over. If you somehow found some ancient one at death's door and thought you'd drink your way to the top,
it was much more likely that they'd simply hijack your body. They were novels. He didn't know that any of it was true. But from the way they'd been shelved, he thought
he'd been told something. If he went that way, he'd have to go a rung at a time. He couldn't skip ahead.

"I'm planning to be here forever," he said. "And that means I can't just be an outcast. A nobody, with no rights, and no land, and nothing, forever. We play our cards
right. Somewhere between getting older, getting more potent blood, maybe blood alchemy's got some tricks to give us the full powers. Maybe we can steal power from people
who screw up. We've got to keep moving up the chain. I won't get swept aside."

## Simon

There was a man at the bail bonds office that week who'd been picked up for assault and battery.

He'd been drinking in a bar, and seen a man across the room whom, both of them agreed, he'd never met. He'd walked over with his beer, smashed the bottle over the
man's head, thrown him on the floor, and started stomping on him. It had taken three people to pull him off. His name was Simon.

And he knew something. He'd been somebody's ghoul, maybe, once. Nothing had hold of him now, no compulsion that Ethan could see. But when Ethan walked past, Simon
looked at him with a familiarity that was almost presumptuous, and got belligerent.

"What, I'm not good enough for you? You don't want to make me one of your little minions?"

"What are you talking about?"

"All you biters seem intent on having a little pack following you around. Like lapdogs."

"I don't have anything like that. What are you talking about? How do you know this?"

"Everybody knows this. You bite them, and they do whatever you tell them."

That wasn't Ethan's understanding. Rose didn't keep ghouls, but he'd seen other people's, and it wasn't that simple or that total. This guy was making one bad
experience into a law of the universe.

"Come with me," said Ethan.

He put everything he had into it, and it worked the way it sometimes did: terror in Simon's eyes, and his body entirely compliant. Simon straightened up and marched
behind him to the basement. Ethan locked the door behind them, with the key; the doors down there locked on both sides.

"I need you to tell me exactly how you know all these things."

Simon broke. He started to cry.

"I miss him so much. He abandoned me. He's the ugliest, cruellest person I've ever met. But Vincent *abandoned* me. And I don't know what to do. I'm trying to find
him. Or just anyone. To make me feel the way I felt."

*I might have found an opportunity*, Ethan thought. "Who else knows what you know?"

"Lots of people. Vincent had five or six. There was Anne Marie, and Linh, and Tony. I don't remember the other names."

"Do you have a crew?"

Simon shut right back up. He straightened again.

"How did you meet Vincent?"

Simon started to say something, and Ethan watched something inside his head stop him.

*Never mind that*, Ethan thought. He ran it back and forth. A Masquerade breach, or an opportunity? Both. Either way, this guy couldn't keep running around doing dumb
shit, and he couldn't keep running his mouth.

He knew a little about how you made a ghoul. You fed them some of your vitae. It usually took three feedings to really lock it in, but even after the first they were very
susceptible to you. And the longer it went on, the small doses over time, the more could develop, up to the point that some of them could use a little of what their
Kindred could.

"If you want to feel like that," he told Simon, "I think I know a way to make it happen. But you'll owe me. I'll cover your bail. I'll see what I can do about making those
charges go away. You'll owe me."

There was a community-service route he could use: anger management, the hours, and Ethan responsible for him for the duration. If Simon got into more trouble, it would come
back on Ethan. But it would work.

He told Rose he'd taken the initiative.

She gave him a very odd look. "Well. I suppose since you've gone to all this effort, you can at least give it a try."

"It's either that or we have to kill him."

"Humor me. Let's try to bond him together. We'll each give him a little of our vitae. Perhaps he'll be responsive to you as well. At the very least, he'll be responsive
to me."

They did it over a couple of nights. It took for Rose. It didn't take for Ethan. Simon was bound to her now.

## Gamut

Gamut messaged Olly about a show. They still liked each other's posts; Olly was still in the scene a little, more in the background than he used to be. She was excited: an
old military warehouse converted into a venue, some big names coming through, the Illenium kind of big, and she was opening.

Olly liked to be in the crowd, though he could go backstage. He brought Trieste.

Backstage, before, Gamut said hi to Trieste and asked if she could borrow Olly for a minute.

"Sure. What's up?"

"I didn't realize mob movies were real."

"What's that, man?"

"I owe a guy a lot of money. He says he's going to break my legs. I kind of believe him." She was being straight with him. "This gig is fantastic for my career. It pays
shit. I'm trying to figure out ways to come up with a lot of money really quick that aren't going to burn me worse than I'm already burned. You seem to have a knack for
finding weird opportunities."

"My ear's not really to the ground right now."

"Okay. If you think of anything in the next week, tell me."

"Yeah. Of course."

Then it was game face and pleasantries, and she excused herself.

Out in the crowd there was the thing a rave does: subwoofers moving your body for you, people all around, an energy you just absorb. For a moment, a brief moment, Olly almost
felt like he wasn't dead.

Then the moment passed. He and Trieste slipped out before the end.

After, he found Gamut. "Short of finding the money. Do you want help with protection?"

"Is that a thing you can offer?"

"On the down-low? I'm a little more in the protection field than the making-money field these days."

"That sounds great." The Thursday after, she said, a couple of nights after the Elysium, as it turned out. "Can you come to my place? And we'll go meet them together. Come to
terms. Maybe they'll do a payment plan."

"I'll be there."

## Best Behaviour

Ralph fed every night that week. It wasn't his pattern; he was breaking it on purpose. He couldn't bring himself to kill anyone, and he wanted to walk into that room as far
from hungry as he could get. So: a little, a lot of times. A sip here, a sip there, half of it in the old neighbourhood and half in the new, to spread the impact. It turned out
to feel very sustainable. There was always an edge. But it stayed at the edge, somewhere in the periphery, where he could distract himself from it.

He had his Tom Ford suit dry-cleaned, and his Omega Speedmaster polished, and wore the custom-tooled Italian shoes. He did not bring Bill.

Ethan wore the kind of suit you buy off the rack and have taken in a little. It said *belongs here*. It didn't make anybody turn round.

Olly wore his less-scuffed leather jacket, and khakis, and a flannel shirt, because it had buttons, and that seemed dressy.

---

It was one of those rentable rooms at the University, the kind corporate events and weddings use, fancy in a different way from the chantry. A hundred people all told, which
did and didn't feel like a lot. Most of them were ghouls. Fewer than half were Kindred.

At the door was the Sheriff.

He recognized Olly at once, which was not surprising, as Olly had hit him with a car. He gave him a wide smile that was pure malice.

"On your *best* behaviour, now."

Olly couldn't leave it. "Seen a guy on fire recently? Any idea where he went?"

There were candles around the edges of the room, muted, tended, the kind of thing nobody frenzies over. The Sheriff reached over and took a candelabrum, and held it up right in
front of Olly's face, the flames inches from his eyes.

"Burned," said the Sheriff. "To a crisp. Just like you will be."

He held it there. Daring Olly to flinch. Daring him to move.

Olly had watched eight new vampires go up in ash less than a week before. He knew exactly what fire could do. He also knew it had never done it to him, and that the Sheriff was
bound by the same rules everyone else was here tonight. If something happened to Olly, in here, that would be the Sheriff's problem.

The thing inside Olly took that and said: *go on. Fucking try it.*

And pushed him forward. Olly felt the heat of the flames on his skin, and kept coming, and the Sheriff had to pull the candelabrum back, and then pull it back again, and then take
a step backward, and then another. Finally, he just moved out of the way.

Olly gave him a salute and walked past.

Behind him, Ralph and Ethan had watched all of it. Ethan's opinion of Olly went up considerably. *All right. That was pretty badass.*

The Sheriff waved the two of them through. *Yeah. You're good.*

---

There were people in a lounge to one side who were not ghouls: four or five of them sitting there, periodically looking up and around the room with a kind of invitation. A tasting,
of sorts.

At one of the tables a man was holding court, literally and otherwise. It looked casual. People came over; some sat down, some introduced themselves standing; and when he gave a
little flick of the wrist, that part of the conversation was over, and they got up and went, and others came to fill the space.

Rose was sitting with Simon. She waved them over, and gave them the who's who. Evelyn they had met, though never for long; Rose had been keeping her up to date on the work with Dominik.
Father Vivek was at a different table with three people who wore matching scarves, a distinctive teal, and a scarf of his own, more casual, but the same, and they were very close to
him. And Sabine.

"At some point tonight," said Rose, "you should go up to Sabine and congratulate her on, as always, a wonderful Elysium."

Sabine was the Keeper of Elysium. If this were a wedding, she'd be the planner: everything went smoothly, and violence stayed outside.

Ethan added it to his mental to-do list.

## Vincent

Ralph was looking for someone extremely ugly.

He worked the room systematically, trying to look like he wasn't, and he found what he was looking for. Someone went off with the Prince into a separate room, for a private
conversation, and it was that face, or near enough.

He lingered near the door, discreetly. He tried to hear, and couldn't.

Was it his sire? He wasn't sure. He'd had him for about an hour, on the night he was made, before he was dropped. It had been a stressful night. *Either way*, he thought, *he's
of my bloodline, one way or another.*

---

Ethan went and paid his respects to Sabine.

She was just finishing with somebody, and turned, and there he was.

"Hello, yes. Ethan. I don't think we've met. Rose's childe, yes?"

"Yes. I just wanted to say I really appreciate the hospitality. It's a lovely evening. Thank you."

"I love courteous guests. You are most welcome here." A pause. "Rose surprises me sometimes. Her judgement keeps her over in Evelyn's camp, and I can't say that doesn't sting a
little. I feel she'll come round one of these days. But she seems to be trying to do right by you. However uphill that may be."

"She's been pretty good to me. Seems right to return the favour as best I can."

"Well," said Sabine. "I do hope you don't suffer the same fate as her previous childe."

"She hasn't spoken much about her previous childe."

"Probably better left forgotten."

Ethan went away very confused. It was, he'd admit, not hard to do. The single beleaguered hamster in his head was on its wheel.

---

The three of them bumped into each other for a moment.

"I think I spotted my sire," said Ralph. "I think he's meeting with the Prince. I'm going to try and talk to him. Figure some things out. Maybe do some networking."

"You should talk to him," said Olly. "I can't imagine if I didn't have Rami." He looked across the room. "And I should probably introduce myself to the Prince."

"I think that's one of the Traditions," said Ralph.

"He might not kill me on sight. That's good." Olly had a thought. "Have you guys seen any Tzimisce? It occurred to me they could be here."

He'd been looking. He'd been looking for hardened, elongated features, extra cartilage, that kind of thing, which was all he'd picked up about them. The closest thing he'd seen
was someone talking with Evelyn, the two of them going over a book together: an androgynous face, skin almost translucent, the veins showing more than on anybody he'd ever seen.
Some sort of Nosferatu, probably. It didn't seem like a match. He hadn't gone over.

"From the little research we've done," said Ralph, "I don't think you can spot one. The whole point is fleshcrafting. They could look like anyone."

"That's not how I remembered it," said Olly. "Well. How do I know you're Ralph and not a Tzimisce?"

"You don't."

"All right. Well. I can't get drunk at this party. So I'll just go find somewhere to sit down."

One of the vessels tapped him on the shoulder. "I heard you wanted to get drunk."

Olly looked at the vessel. "Do you have something?"

"Do you want alcohol, or coke?"

"All right, guys," said Olly. "I'll see you later." He followed the vessel. *This is a horrible idea*, he thought. *I love it.*

One of the vessels was very drunk and one was very high, and it came through when you fed. Olly had both. He got a speedball going, and he was tweaking a little, but he could pilot
it through to the end of the night, and he had a really nice buzz on, and now he was making friends with everybody.

---

The Nosferatu came out of the private room, and a little after, the Prince came out and went back to his table.

On his way past, the Nosferatu looked at Ralph, and there was recognition, immediate. He shook his head.

"What do you want of me?"

"My name's Ralph. We haven't been formally introduced, I believe."

"Yes. I suppose. Vincent."

"What I'm looking for is an introduction. We share a bloodline. I wish to entangle myself in it as much as possible."

"My bloodline ends with me," said Vincent. "What you have, I don't know what it is you have. You have your looks still, that's for sure. You don't have anything that is recognizably
mine."

"Didn't I have your blood? With time and effort I'll grow more potent. And perhaps I'll lose my looks. In the meantime, I have my looks. I may be of use."

Vincent gave him a really strange look.

"Do you have a place?"

"Yes. I have domain."

"You have—" And Vincent put it together. "You're those thin-bloods. Barbara Hall Park."

"Correct."

"Well. *Admiration* feels too strong a word. You have exceeded my expectations. Considerably." He considered Ralph again. "How do you occupy yourself, these nights?"

"Currently I'm running errands for the chantry."

"For Dominik. And he pays you well?"

"He affords me something less than admiration. But better than disdain."

"That is possibly the highest you will get from him. But it isn't nothing." Vincent was quiet a moment. "I'm a little down on my luck, right now. I don't know that there's a good use I
could put you to. Or a way to pay you for it. What I can do is: if you wish to visit me."

He told Ralph where. Down on the harbourfront, by Ireland Park, just before you get to the island airport.

"I generally keep to myself. I don't usually come to these things. I had some pressing business. Someone destroyed my haven. I have to rebuild."

"That is unfortunate. If I can be of assistance, let me know."

"You're staying at Barbara Hall Park?"

"At times. I spend my time in multiple places. To reduce exposure."

"If you don't pay me a visit in the next few weeks," said Vincent, "I will come and find you, and pay you one. I'm intrigued to learn how you've managed to stay alive thus far. Or
maintained your unlife."

"Thank you," said Ralph. "And—have you seen this face before?"

He showed him the drawing.

Vincent looked at it, and shuddered.

"That is a cursed face. You do not wish to fight that face."

"Alas, I am bidden to. He has meddled in my affairs, and the affairs of the chantry. I'm to retrieve something he stole."

"Then the next best thing I can offer you is this. You should speak to Baron Skye. Baron Evelyn. She may have some resources you could leverage. He is very old, and powerful—"

"Mind you," said Ralph, "we can endure some degree of daylight. That gives us some advantages. Thanks for the information. I look forward to seeing you in a few nights."

And Vincent left. Not the conversation: the Elysium.

*A lead*, thought Ralph. *Excellent.*

## The Room

Ethan tried to read the room. Everyone in here, at the end of the day, was some kind of criminal dealing with other criminals, and that he understood, even if the society
itself was mostly over his head. Who talked to whom. Body language. Where the power sat.

One of the teal scarves found him: a young woman, white, Canadian, early twenties, polite. The temple, she said, was having a party for New Year's. They'd be welcome. The
address she gave him, when he looked it up on his phone, was not a church; it was a community centre. It wasn't Elysium, she said; the Masquerade would need to be kept. But it would
be very Kindred-friendly. Bring your friends.

"Thanks. We'll certainly try to stop by."

As for the room, he couldn't get a read on it. Elysiums weren't regular. People used them to cycle through everybody at once, five minutes each, like a family reunion. The groups
churned constantly. A few things lasted longer: Evelyn and the person with the book; Father Vivek and his acolytes, before they dispersed; and everybody, at one point or another,
going up to the Prince to pay respects and ask for things. But the whole thing ran less than four hours from start to finish, and nobody arrived on time, and people left early. He
couldn't tell who the cliques were, or who was at odds with whom. It was too formal, and too short.

He did bump into Olly. Olly had been going around telling people that when you're Kindred it can be hard to keep up a social life, but raves, you know, happen at night. He was
playing drum and bass off his phone at people, who were asking him to turn it off. If he got deep into a conversation with anyone, he'd bring up that his DJ friend was trying to make
some extra money, and did anyone have any leads? He was talking too much to everybody.

Ethan was aware the Sheriff was still in the room.

"Ethan! Haven't seen you in a while, what's up?"

"You need to get a handle on yourself," Ethan said, low. "There might not be violence here. That doesn't mean you can't make a mistake that'll kill you."

"All right. Okay. I'll talk to you later."

Ethan turned away from the train wreck and went to talk to people who would not get him killed.

---

Ralph went to Sabine.

She looked at him closely. "I must confess, I was expecting you to look a little different. You look much better than I expected."

"Well, hopefully, with time and effort, my looks will fade. But I'm cursed as I am now. Not that I'm complaining. How are you finding the party?"

"Oh, I'm loving it so far." Keeper of Elysium was her role, she reminded him; she ought to.

"Given the thinness of my blood," said Ralph, "and how tenuous my connection is to our greater society here. Would it be presumptuous, or appropriate, for me to present myself to the
Prince?"

"Excellent question," said Sabine. "How about I introduce you?"

"That would be delightful."

"Come with me."

She hooked her arm through his and walked him over, and cut off whoever was talking to the Prince at that moment. "Excuse me. Lucian, darling, there's someone I want you to meet. This
is Ralph, correct? Ralph Begg. Vincent's childe, I believe."

Ralph didn't correct her.

"Ah, yes," said the Prince. "Vincent. Vincent and I were speaking at length." He extended his hand, and Ralph took it. "You have impeccable connections, it seems. Mind your manners and
you will have a good stay."

"Thank you, my liege."

The Prince turned his head back to the conversation he'd been having. Sabine steered Ralph away.

"Well," she said. "That went pretty well, all things considered. He didn't kill you."

"That's a good start."

"It's something of a first."

"My goal," said Ralph, "is to be remembered, but not thought of too much, by those in power."

"That is an excellent habit to cultivate. If you're ever interested in helping with the next Elysium, you have exactly the right mindset for it. I'd be happy to indulge in a little
cross-training."

Ralph placed himself at her service.

---

Ethan went back to Rose, and gave her a quick rundown of his night, and asked what she thought about him introducing himself to anyone. The friends. The Sheriff.

"To what end?"

"Just a pleasant introduction. One that doesn't involve me dying again."

"I would suggest," said Rose, "that if we're going to introduce you to anyone, we attempt the Prince. Together."

She stood and took his hand and led him forward. There was a short queue. It took about fifteen minutes to cycle through the people ahead of them, and Ethan watched every one of them, how
they came up and how they left, and what the Prince did with each, and by the time they got to the front he thought he knew what to do.

"Prince," said Rose. "You may or may not remember me. I'm associated with Baron Evelyn. This is my childe. We are performing some work for the chantry, which we hope will be mutually beneficial.
I wanted to introduce you to Ethan here, and make sure you were aware that we are working to shared purposes." She looked at Ethan. "Did you want to add anything about your work with Dominik?"

"We're making progress," said Ethan. "Demonstrable results. Sooner rather than later is our goal."

Lucian looked up at him. "It is my understanding that you have been given Barbara Hall Park as a domain."

"Yes. And we're working to make sure it's strong."

"Keep that in mind. Thank you for making me aware. You may go."

What Ethan heard under it was: *Thank you for informing me. I will not be going to that park.* Given that Rose belonged to a Baron, and everything else, that was the height of courtesy. It was
about as good as it could have gone.

## Siblings

After, the three of them compared notes.

"So," said Ralph. "Guys. Do you want to help me kill my sire?"

Olly stared at him.

"Pros and cons," said Ralph. "If we're going to make ourselves more potent, we need potent blood. He's got it. He doesn't seem particularly well connected, or well suited to much of anything.
He's semi-homeless right now. He looks a bit desperate. Strike while the iron's hot."

"You're suggesting diablerie?" said Olly.

Ethan knew a little about diablerie. Mostly that it was the thing you did not do. "I don't think we need to engage in that practice."

"There has to be a way, using all this alchemy stuff, to use potent blood to make us more powerful. Blood is power."

"I don't think that's how it works," said Olly, and he knew better than either of them. He had tasted thin-blood concoctions made with the vitae of Kindred who weren't thin-blooded. Some of them
were no joke. All of them were temporary. The longest anything lasted, that he knew of, was a month after you took it. And the shelf life varied. Some spoiled in days or weeks, and you needed
them fresh. Others were sold at the COAG as supposedly hundreds of years old. It depended on what was in them. Some were like fine wine. Some were like mayonnaise.

"If you don't think it'll work," said Ralph, "I understand. But there's got to be something we'd get out of it. Listen. Rose seems great. Your sire seems fine. This isn't some generalized need
to deal with Elders. This is specific to my case." He shrugged. "I'll meet with him. Check it out. See if it looks like a good opportunity."

Then Ethan heard the name properly, and it mattered.

"Your sire's name is Vincent." He had a story that might sound familiar. "My guy Simon. He was a ghoul. Abandoned by a vampire named Vincent. The ugliest,
cruellest person he'd ever met, he said."

There weren't that many Kindred. It was very likely the same Vincent.

"That ghoul could be a valuable source of information," said Ralph.

"Just think of all the long-lost siblings you might find," said Olly.

"If we're going to go after this guy, we should pump the ghoul for information. As much as possible. I think he'll be pretty cooperative."

"Also," said Ralph, "Vincent said Evelyn might know more about our Tzimisce friend. She's connected to Rose. Could you ask Rose to set up a meeting at some point?"

"I can do that," said Ethan. "I've got a bone to pick with Rose myself."

Olly had his own list. He'd heard a lot tonight about what an athanor could hold. He wanted to know what had been brewing in him. And maybe they could get their hands on something powerful.

So: Simon, about Vincent. Rose, about Evelyn, and about whatever it was Sabine had meant. Rami, about what was inside Olly. The harbourfront. And Gamut, on Thursday.

On the whole, it had gone well. Olly had made a certain impression on the Sheriff. Ralph and Ethan had both survived meeting the Prince. Sometimes that's where the bar is. They had cleared it.
