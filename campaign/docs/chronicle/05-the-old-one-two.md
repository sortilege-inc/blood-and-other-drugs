## Lab Rats

Olly called Rami on the move, half a block behind the Sheriff.

Rami picked up, which was its own kind of luck. "Say your piece."

Olly said it badly. The Sheriff. Friends of his. Something about a Ravnos stash, and trouble for it, and
somebody called Tash at the other end.

"You," said Rami, "are a prime fucking piece of lab equipment. Do not mess around with the Sheriff. What are you
doing?"

"Some friends are getting dragged toward execution. Do you know the executioner in this area?"

There was a pause while Rami worked out what he'd actually been told. The Sheriff hadn't killed them where they
stood. He wasn't making an example of them in the street. He was taking them somewhere.

"Tash," Rami said, when Olly gave him the name. "That would be Tatiana Mirek. She is part of a chantry. Over by
Wellesley. The Queen's Park area." Another pause. "That's probably worse. Your friends are going to be lab
rats."

He asked what Olly owed them. Olly said they were close with Trieste.

"So, don't care. They made a mistake. You should not make the same mistake."

He left a silence, the kind he used to give you one chance to be stupid out loud. Olly didn't take it. Rami was
about to hang up. Then, instead:

"Do you have any concoctions on you?"

"I just got one."

"What's the flavour?"

"Melancholic. He calls it the Strength of Sadness."

"Yes," Rami said. "Yes. That would be—" And he stopped himself, and Olly could hear him stop. "No. Just forget
they existed. That's not your problem."

The line went dead.

Olly knew why Rami cared, the little that he did. There had been one before him. Another thin-blood, another
furnace, and that one had been destroyed, and Rami had had to go out and find a replacement. The replacement was
Olly. Rami was careful with his tools.

---

He kept following.

Queen's Park he knew. He hadn't been up there much since he died, but he knew the way, and he knew the quick way,
and he knew he could get there before the Sheriff did. What he didn't know was what to do once he was there. Wait for
Trieste, and let them get behind whatever door they were going behind, where a rescue would be ten times harder. Or
try something now, alone.

The Sheriff didn't hurry. He didn't have to. He had Lil Sis and Malik by the arms, lifted just enough that their feet
barely touched the pavement, the way a grown man walks a little kid who doesn't want to go. They were nominally
walking. They weren't going anywhere he didn't want.

He was huge. Shooting him from a rooftop seemed like a bet. Olly looked at the cars on the street instead.

At that time of night the problem wasn't finding one. It was starting one. But Olly had some expertise there, and
some time. He picked an SUV. He got it going. He took the gun out of his bag and put it on the seat beside him, in
case whatever came after the collision needed finishing.

There was the question of the other two. The Sheriff had one of them in each hand. Hitting the middle and missing the
edges was not a thing anyone could do with a car. Hitting two, and choosing which one took less of it: that was
possible. It was a trolley problem, and Olly had about a block to solve it.

Lil Sis, he decided. She looked less squishy.

He let them pass. Then he hit the high beams, for the theatre of it, so the big man would have a second to wonder what
was happening, and put his foot down.

## The Chantry

On the other side of the same stretch of night, Rose was driving Ethan and Ralph to meet a Tremere.

Ethan had heard of the Tremere. Everybody talked a little smack about the Tremere, even when they did it with fear and
trembling. A magical order, the story went, older than it had any right to be; not even vampires to begin with,
people said, until they'd found some sorcerous way to graft themselves into the blood. Anything occult came back round
to them sooner or later. They kept houses for it, chantries, and some of those were buildings and some were more like a
university department: a clade of researchers sharing books and methods. They also tended to sit close to power. Which
was why Ethan had never met one. He'd spent his nights so far staying out of the Camarilla's part of the city.

Rose parked by the library. Phones and anything else with a battery went into the glove compartment.

"Let me do the talking," she said. She looked at Ralph. "You have it on you?"

"Of course."

The door was in the side of one of the old stone buildings: heavy, iron, the kind of door that looks like it was
retired when the building got modernized and somebody moved the real entrance round the corner. Rose reached up and took
hold of the big handle, which looked like a plug, and pried it off with both hands. It was a magnet. She set it against
a different part of the door, and something inside clicked softly, and she pulled the door open on a very dim light. She
put the plug back and waved them in ahead of her.

It was a wacky door. Ethan kept that to himself. Head on a swivel. Pay attention. Don't put your foot in your
mouth. Watch Rose for any sign that he was about to fuck something up.

Up three flights. A gallery overlooking a garden, a real one, growing in the middle of the building. Past it, a short
stair to what should have been a service level, storage, pipes. Instead there was carpet, beautiful carpet, and paintings,
and soft light, and music from somewhere. A ghoul in good clothes opened the doors and took their names and offered to
take their coats. It was like one of those clubs styled on a century or two ago. There were drinks, a formality; there
was something in them that was probably bagged blood, not fresh, but enough to give it a little vim.

And there were books. Tomes open on wooden stands, incredibly thick. Strange devices of brass set at particular angles,
that looked like they might be tracking planets, or something else.

Ralph slowed down at the books. He had Spanish and English and Russian, and he wanted to know if any of this was in
one of them. Most of it he couldn't even place the alphabet. One he nearly could: letters like Cyrillic, arranged in no
pattern he'd ever seen. The way Farsi wears the Arabic script, it was a script he knew on a language he didn't. He stood
over it anyway, and moved his lips a little, as if reading a passage, in case one word stuck that he could drop into
conversation later.

The man who came to meet them introduced himself as Dominik Levesque. He shook Rose's hand.

"Welcome. I do believe you have an offer of sorts. Please, let us see this device."

---

Ralph had been playing the shell game all night. The Pelican case, the jacket, the case again. He held out his hand to
Ethan for the case now, and opened it, and if anybody in that room noticed that the bone had not been in the case until
the moment it came out of the case, nobody's face said so.

He looked at it again himself, in that light, in that room. It looked like what it had always looked like. A human femur.
The strange writing on it.

He set it down on a cloth on the table, with some ceremony. You can always wash a cloth.

Dominik bent to it, all the way at the waist, and peered at it from very close. He didn't touch it. He ran his fingers a
few millimetres above its surface, all along its length, and talked to himself too quietly to follow.

Ethan listened to the building. Apart from the four of them there was nobody in the room. The ghoul was down the hall.
Every door they'd passed stood open, rooms running into rooms, and he hadn't seen or heard another soul.

"Initial assessments," Dominik said at last, straightening. "I think your guess is correct. This seems to be authentic.
My estimation would be that this is *Tzimisce Haudenosaunee*."

He let it sit. "Obviously we would need more time. But what you are looking at is probably an incredibly potent spell. A
curse. Something of that nature. Where did you get this, again?"

"There was quite a tale involved in its acquisition," said Ralph. "Possibly a dangerous tale."

Dominik looked at him properly for the first time. "I'm sorry. Who are you?"

"My name's Ralph. I'm new."

Dominik looked at Rose, then back. "You are under her protection?"

"Yes," said Ralph.

"I see." He turned back to her as if Ralph had been answered and filed. "Well, Rose. As I was saying, this seems quite
useful, and valuable. Here we have the means to study it and protect it. My offer: I have a neighbourhood not far from
here. Barbara Hall Park, near Church Street. Gloucester. There has not been as much activity there of late. The
Camarilla could use someone holding down the fort. If you want it as your feeding grounds, I will grant it to you, and my
protection, and your passage in Camarilla territory. And if you wish to extend that to those under your protection, I
suppose I won't prevent it. Do those terms seem acceptable?"

Rose shook on it without looking at either of them.

"Do you need anything while we're here?" she asked them, in the tone of someone who very much hoped not. Transacted.
Done. Get the fuck out. "We'll come back in the near future and see what you've learned."

---

The iron door shut behind them, and they could talk again.

"Well," said Rose. "That could have gone a lot worse."

"I'll take your word for that," said Ralph. "I didn't realize our status was quite that low."

"Now, you didn't hear this from me, and do not say this to him." She kept walking. "Word has it he tried to sire a
childe, and it turned out thin-blooded, and he had it killed. So there was a reason I took the precautions I did."

Ralph thought about his own sire. "Mine did something similar, I think. Broke my neck.
Left me, I assume, to die." He considered it. "They probably should have known that wouldn't do it. So maybe it was meant
more as an insult."

"But the good news." Rose almost smiled. "That's territory we've never had. Ours, with permission. Full writ. I've never
been a huge fan of the Camarilla. But if they're going to give us ground we can feed on without fear of retaliation, I'll
take it. And I'm very willing to extend my protection to you two, with it."

They were most of the way back to the car when they heard the engine and the screech.

## Point Blank

Olly got most of what he wanted.

The Sheriff heard him coming. There's a speed some of them have, when it comes to it, and he could have been gone before
the bumper arrived. But that meant letting go of his prisoners, and he would not let go. So he took it. The SUV caught
him and Malik together and threw them, into a light post and a garbage can and a mailbox, and Lil Sis was yanked out of
his grip the other way with her arm going somewhere arms don't go. Olly stopped short of the curb and the wall. He grabbed
the gun and rolled out of the car.

Lil Sis was on her feet, arm hanging wrong, screaming, "Malik! Take him! *Take* him!"

The Sheriff had gone over the light post and folded across the mailbox; Malik had hit the mailbox square and bounced off
the wall. Malik was still getting up.

The Sheriff was already up.

And then he moved, and Olly had never seen one of them move like that. There was no between. The Sheriff was by the
mailbox and then he was right in front of Olly with a hand on the gun, and what saved Olly was nothing he decided. Pure
muscle memory: the step back, the gun swapped to the other hand, the big fingers closing on air.

He was right there. Olly put the gun against his head and fired.

It was, he'd think later, like hitting a deer with your car. You get out after, and you finish it, for ethical reasons.

The Sheriff froze. Shook his head once, like a dog shaking off water. And turned back to Olly, ready to go.

Olly's scream changed in the middle. It started out as the kind you scream at a man you're shooting in the face and
finished as the other kind.

---

Ethan and Ralph came up on it from the dark side of the street. Rose had taken one look and gone for the car, and on her
way she'd told Ethan: *get close enough to see what's going on. Don't get involved unless you have to.* Ethan had put
himself between her and the noise anyway, because that was the job, and slid an extendable baton out of his pocket, and
kept it closed in his fist. He had several at the office. Every once in a while, people need a little encouragement.

Ralph looked for anything bulletproof: a low wall, a planter, some piece of civic statuary. Cover first. Watching second.

What they saw was a man get shot in the face and shrug it off.

It is a little intimidating, watching that.

Then Lil Sis pulled herself up to her full height and put everything she had into five words.

"I want you to stop!"

And the Sheriff did.

He turned toward her, and it was something longer than the bullet: a tug of war with nothing on the rope, the big man
straining at some invisible thing and losing, for now. He didn't move.

Olly backed away. Shooting didn't work. He'd shot the guy in the head, and the guy was fine. He needed space between
himself and whatever this was, and to watch, and understand.

Malik came up behind the Sheriff swigging from a little flask, or something that looked like one. He took a lighter out
of his pocket. He lit it, and held it in front of his face, and *licked* it, and fire filled his mouth and ran down his
throat like it lived there. He wrapped the Sheriff up from behind in a bear hug and breathed it out, all over him.

Both of them started to burn.

All three watching felt the thing in them that wanted to run. Ralph and Ethan held it down. Olly held it down too, and
then did something worse, from the point of view of self-preservation. He took his coat off.

*It's the right thing to do.* They were his friends, sort of. He was responsible for Malik,
sort of. He beat at the fire with the coat and tried to smother it. The Sheriff was going to be pretty messed up after
being on fire; there were three of them; they could handle the Sheriff. That was the math.

The fire went down. Malik didn't want it down. He was working against Olly, feeding it, and Olly could feel the heat
coming off him from the inside.

Across the street Ethan and Ralph were observing, and mentally taking notes. It was not their fight.

Then the Sheriff arched back and shook, and whatever Lil Sis had on him came loose.

Olly got hold of Malik with both hands. He felt the pull start, the same impossible pull as before, and held on as hard as
he'd ever held anything, and Malik's grip on the Sheriff's neck held harder, and what Olly kept was a handful of scorched
clothing. The rest of Malik was gone.

A shape went past, between the place Ethan was hiding and the place Ralph was hiding. It stopped at the chantry's iron
door. The Sheriff was there, Malik still on his back, still tussling, and the Sheriff was hammering on the door with his
fist, *wham, wham, wham*.

Then the door was closed and nobody was in front of it.

---

Rose had the car running in the library lot. She hadn't moved it.

Lil Sis ran toward the door, and Olly ran after Lil Sis still holding the smoking rags of Malik's clothes, and in doing it
passed right between Ethan and Ralph without seeing either of them. A window came down as he passed the car. Rose looked
out at him, oddly.

"That's suicide," she said to Lil Sis. "Get in the car." The other window came down. "Ethan. Ralph. Get in the car."

Lil Sis looked from the car to the door and back. She cursed for a while. Then she got in.

Rose drove, and Ralph took shotgun, because he was important, and Ethan got in the back with the other two because they
were exactly the kind of people he wasn't going to turn his back on.

"So who the fuck are you," said Rose, backing out, "and why are you attacking the Sheriff in the middle of the street in
the middle of the night? What the fuck are you thinking?"

"You can't allow these abductions," Olly said. "Under what justice is this person just taking my friends?"

"That's the Sheriff, and we're in Camarilla territory. Do you want to take it up with the Prince?" To Lil Sis: "What did
your friends do? What did you do?"

"I didn't do anything. We found that shit from the Ravnos cache and we were at the COAG to see if we could pawn it off.
Besides. Fuck the Camarilla."

"That's what I'm saying," said Olly.

"You might want to mind your voice in here," said Rose. "We just got gifted territory from the Camarilla, and I'd kind of
like to keep it, if you don't mind."

Lil Sis stared at her. "Who'd you have to suck off to get territory from *them*?"

"We gave someone a good donor."

"A good *boner*," said somebody, and that was the word that stuck.

"My stop's a little closer," said Lil Sis, when they came to it. "Thanks for the ride. It won't happen again."

## Barbara Hall Park

Rose parked on the street by the park, their park now, and checked her watch: an hour and a half before the start of
sunup. "Shall we scope it out? See what the feeding's like. Meet back here in an hour."

She went off one way. That left Olly and Ethan and Ralph standing on the sidewalk.

"Guys," Olly said. "I'm not normally caught up in these. I'm not normally just in the street shooting at people. You caught
me at a weird time."

"Well," said Ralph, "you sounded like you weren't super comfortable."

"That guy was invincible. Did you see that? I only shot him once."

"I've never seen anything like that," said Ralph.

"You haven't?"

"Not exactly. I'd kind of figured out that we can't really be hurt easily. But getting shot in the face would probably hurt
me quite a bit. I don't want to test it."

"Good policy," said Ethan.

"Ralph has the most to lose in the getting-shot-in-the-face department," Olly said. "Handsome fellow that he is."

"That's the moneymaker."

Olly kept worrying at it. "It didn't seem like a good idea to go after the Sheriff, right? My friends, in that building.
You guys were inside that building. Seemed pretty secure."

"Going after the Sheriff," said Ethan, "pretty fucking stupid, man. You attacked him and he saw your face."

"I would've killed him. But you could have killed him. You were right there."

"Yes. But I didn't attack him. I'm not worried about retaliation from him."

"You should be," said Ralph.

"I'm going to be honest," said Olly. "I drove into him with a car. I thought that was going to handle the whole thing. The
gun was like, if I needed a finishing blow. You ever hit a deer with your car?"

"It's happened."

"Usually you've got to go out and finish the animal off. For ethical reasons. I shot that man in the head."

---

In daylight, if any of them ever saw it in daylight, it was a neighbourhood of low buildings, two and three and four stories.
Shops at street level. Apartments over the shops. A few proper apartment buildings. Almost no houses.

Ethan was already thinking about real estate. A building, or part of one, that he could lease. Move his work over here: it
was his business, he could do that. Staff there in the day, keeping an eye on things, and a ready explanation for the parts
that were hardened. *Why have you got a steel door and a whole bunch of non-lethal weapons lying around?* This is for work.
It's a legitimate business.

He walked it with a notebook, taking down the numbers on the for-rent signs. One caught his eye: a door between two
shopfronts, the kind that goes up to apartments, with a slot too narrow to reach through. Through the slot he could see
stairs going up, and stairs going down. Commercial at street level, maybe residential above, maybe a basement below. That
would be perfect. He'd need somebody to make calls during business hours, and one of his people could do the walkthrough
and hold up a phone for a video tour.

Ralph looked at who lived here, because that was what a hunting ground was. The low apartments had grilles and grates on
their windows, the kind that open from inside in a fire and not from outside. Getting in would be the question. But once in,
there was an embarrassment of riches: floor on floor of people asleep. He preferred his own ground. This would do as a
backup.

Olly went up. He did his urban jumping to the top of a short building and looked out at the city, mournfully, and called
Trieste, and explained that they'd lost Malik.

"I showed up and everyone was gone," she said. "Where are you? Where's Lil Sis?"

"Lil Sis is with us. It got really hot, and the Sheriff went inside a building, and some random person offered us a ride.
Like, a vampire. So we took it."

"You just accept rides from strangers?"

"It was a trying situation."

"All right. What's the plan with Malik? Is he dead?"

"Word from Rami is *lab rats*. So we better hope he's dead, because the alternatives are worse. On the bright side, last time
I saw him he was on fire."

"But you're okay. Lil Sis is okay."

"Yeah. We got out. I'm at this park. In Church–Wellesley."

"I'll be right over."

---

She found Ralph first, checking the grates on a basement window. She kicked him gently behind the knee so his leg buckled a
little.

"Are you the one that picked up my boy?"

"Depends who your boy is."

"Mousy looking. Dark hair, kind of long. Olly. Likes to shoot people."

"Oh," said Ralph, "is he shooting people now?"

"Apparently."

"That's fun. Can you lead the way? By the way, I'm Ralph."

"Ralph." She shook his hand. "How do you like your nights?"

"Don't know yet. I'm kind of new to this."

"How do you like your sunrises? You should join us for one."

"For a sunrise? Seems painful."

"Most of us make it back."

"Not a fan of Russian roulette."

"Well, if you ever change your mind," said Trieste, "it's the thrill of a lifetime."

"That," said Ralph, "is what I'm worried about."

Olly came down by the stairs. Climbing up a building was one thing; climbing down one sounded awful, and nobody locks the
roof door from the inside anyway. He found the two of them just as Ralph was declining.

"There's the man of the hour," said Trieste. "I heard you were shooting people now."

"It seemed like the situation called for it. I also hit him with a car."

"Before or after you shot him?"

"Right before."

"The old one-two." She nodded. "Wasn't sufficient, it sounds like."

"He didn't mind getting shot in the head. Or set on fire. Lil Sis seemed to mind-control him for a second. Tough guy."

## King Dre

"So we're not really on speaking terms," said Trieste, walking, "but if anyone can throw down the Sheriff, it's King Dre, over
in Scarborough. I bet we could get him to lend us a few heavies. Set up a trap, or come straight at the Sheriff. We can pay
blood for blood. And we totally should."

She said all of this with Ralph standing right there, a man she had met about four minutes ago. Congratulations, Ralph:
co-conspirator.

"I heard the Prince wouldn't take kindly," said Ralph, mildly.

"Are you scared of the Prince?"

"I haven't had the pleasure of meeting the Prince."

"I think we should talk to King Dredd," said Olly.

"Dre."

"I'm sure he won't object to being called King Dredd."

Ethan heard the tail end of it, walking up. *Are you scared of the Prince?* His eyebrows went up a little. That sounded like a
conversation worth listening to, so he listened. Ralph, beside him, said very little. Smile and nod. Get some heavies, start a
war. He was just being a sponge.

Trieste talked, and dropped names, and Olly listened to her the way he'd learned to listen to her.

The first thing he understood was that the space of things was bigger than he'd realized. Not the number of Kindred; there
weren't that many Kindred in Toronto. Fewer, all told, than a high school. But there were ghouls, and human sympathizers, and
other things that turned up here and there, and it all mattered out of proportion, because you didn't meet many people like
you in a night. They were scattered. They were apex predators. There's a reason you don't see bears going round in herds.

The second thing he understood was that he couldn't tell how much of what she was saying was good. She talked like she knew
everybody. Someone connected with a church of Caine. King Dre. A crew in East York that had fought the Sheriff before, who
didn't like talking to her or to the Sunburners but who she hoped to pull in. And Father Vivek, further north and east, who was
more tolerant of them, only they'd had a falling-out, and making it up would mean something like an apology, which was a hard
sell. She said all of it the same way: *yes, I know these people, I'm very connected.* Some of it, Olly was sure, was made up, or
was someone she'd met once and called a friend, or was an easy sell that wouldn't be easy at all. He just couldn't tell which
parts.

Ethan, listening, could tell more. The names mostly checked out: people and places he'd heard of, or seen, or had from Rose. It
was the connections that didn't. The intentions, the capabilities. And the ones Rose had always seemed closest to, Evelyn and
the others out west, Trieste didn't mention at all.

"Here's an idea," said Olly. "Our new friends Ralph and Ethan here actually have an in with the people the Sheriff's meeting.
They were just in that building. I think they're owed a favour. Maybe we pull some levers there. See if we can get Malik out.
Might be the easiest way through."

Trieste stopped. "Wait. You guys were *working* with them?"

"I don't think *working with* is the right phrasing," said Ralph. "Dealing with is different from working with. And I understand
you want to save your friend. Malik, is it? His plan, in an immediate confrontation, was to set himself and another person on
fire. That was his go-to, under stress. I don't think rescuing him is in his best interests long-term. And certainly not in
yours."

He wasn't lying. That was what made it work.

Trieste looked at him, dumbfounded. "So you're telling me you walked in, and met *Dominik*, and walked out."

"Yes."

"And walked out with *this* territory. Jesus. What did you give him?"

"A knick-knack."

"A boner," said Olly. "I heard."

"*Boner* was the word," Ralph agreed.

She was too stunned to say anything for a moment. "I guess we'll see," she said finally. "So this is yours. Fuck me." Then:
"Can we play here sometimes?"

"As long as you play nice."

"We can play nice. No gunfire."

"No fires. Don't start none."

"That was a one-time thing. Do you know where Lil Sis is?"

"In the car," said Olly.

"I'll go fetch her. See you in a couple of nights. I'll let you know if we learn anything else about the Sheriff." She nodded
at Ralph. "Good to meet you, Ralph." And at Ethan, for about half a second: "Ethan."

"Nice to meet you," said Ethan, to her back.

---

Rose came back shortly after. "Find anything you like?"

"Got my eye on a property," said Ethan. "But I need to talk in private, when you have a minute."

He told her what he'd heard. Trieste, King Dre, heavies, a trap for the Sheriff. "What do you think, boss?"

"I don't fancy their chances," said Rose. "I'm not a fan of selling folks out to the Camarilla. But there could be a major boon in
it."

"I definitely don't want to fight that guy."

"Oh, I'm not touching it with a ten-foot pole. They're not going to do it here, right?"

"They said they'd play nice."

"Then deny all knowledge. Go about your life. If something happens and they get themselves killed, that's on them."

They folded back into the others. Rose turned to Olly.

"You were at that boiler house. Todd's."

"Yeah."

"And you stole all their shit."

"Repossessed. Yes."

"It was yours originally, you could say." She considered him. "So you're in the trade around here. What are you trying to do?
Make? Distribute? Sell?"

"I'm just trying to get the lay of the land. Not long ago I was a daylight guy. I knew a lot about the scene in Toronto, then. I'm
still trying to get a sense of who's buying and selling. But I'm interested in getting involved."

"Then I advise being extremely careful where you do what. Which was the problem with your friend Todd, who did not heed repeated
advisories after multiple infractions." She shrugged. "But if you're looking to make your way, the real money is in artifacts.
Books. The occult shit. Anybody can make potions at this point."

"Can they," said Olly, who had been learning to make potions.

"Valuable to who? It's helpful if you're thin-blooded." She glanced at Ralph and Ethan. "Nothing against thin-bloods. I have no
beef with you whatsoever. But factually, you don't tend to have much in the way of money or boons to offer. So the economy in
selling to you kind of sucks. Stuff with real occult import, though. Now you've got real buyers. Real power. Real boons."

"So I'm a Walter White type," said Olly, "and you're an Indiana Jones. I feel like we walked onto the wrong movie set."

"I'm not going to take any offense," said Ralph. "As you said, none was meant. So clearly there's none for me to take."

She called it a night, and drove them each home: Ralph to the market where he'd left his car, Olly to his place, Ethan last, to
the bail bondsman's. "I've got business for the next few nights," she said to him. "I'll be back by the weekend. You know how to
reach me. I just won't be around."

"I've got a lead on a place," Ethan said. "Might make a good base of operations. My people can make some calls and go by. It looks
good from outside, but that's only half the story."

"Let me know how it goes."

## Two or Three Nights

Bill, the roommate, said hi when Ralph got home. He was still up, for some reason.

Ralph took some of his money out and rented an apartment in the new neighbourhood, a second place, in a building with eleven other
units, which meant building access, which meant floors of them. He'd work out how to use that. He'd work out how to turn Rose's
control of this neighbourhood into *his* control of this neighbourhood, and that into something worth more than five million dollars,
or the trade had been a bad one. It would take time. He had time.

And he needed to stop being thin-blooded. He needed to be a real vampire. He'd have asked for a do-over on the Embrace, if there were

As it turned out, he met someone before that. On the street, one night: a face he remembered from the tunnel. He ran to catch them
up and introduced himself again, and they were brusque and tried to brush him off, and he kept asking. How do I find out when these

hotel. Just make sure you bring a gift.*

It was right at the end that he realized. Either it wasn't true or it was a setup. He was sure it was one or the other; he couldn't
have told you which. Either way it was deeply disrespectful. And the thing inside him that he'd been keeping a lid on said: *fuck these
guys.*

He wasn't a physical guy. He didn't want to burn a bridge he might need. So somebody else would have to suffer for it.

There was a man waiting for a bus. Ralph hit him across the face and kept hitting him. Halfway through, with the man struggling a
little, not much, it occurred to Ralph that this was an opportunity to feed, and he fed. It was a lot more brutal than he usually was.
Nobody was right beside them. There was somebody across the street.

When his head cleared he knew there'd be people coming, police or paramedics, soon. He ran into the night. He was only a couple of blocks
from the new apartment. On the way back it occurred to him that he should be more careful about shitting where he lived and ate.

Ethan's employee brought back a video of the place. The basement was completely below ground: no windows at all, a big selling point for
anyone who dies in sunlight, and the walls down there would be brick or cinderblock against the earth. A place for himself, maybe a bunk
or two, that could be made secure. Somewhere for a holding cell, for the actual work. The ground floor would be the office. The floor
above, storage, and the less public-facing rooms.

It looked like a good location. By a couple of nights later he didn't have the lease yet, but he was sure: this was the place. The paperwork
was grinding. He'd started looking at what else was in the area, and what it meant for advertising.

---

Olly woke the next evening thinking about Trieste.

He'd been going along with her. The rooftops, the sunrises. Tonight she'd been ready to throw her people at the Sheriff, and it had got
one of them taken, and made Olly wonder about her judgement for the first time. And he'd seen the scale of it now: the personal power,
a man who could take a bullet to the head, and the structure over that, the kind that could just *take* people. It bothered him. He didn't
understand it. He didn't think it was right that people disappeared. He wanted to know more, and from where he stood, he couldn't know
anything, or do anything about any of it.

He was hungry. He was pissed off about the Sheriff. He didn't want to call Trieste.

There was an abandoned shopping centre he knew. He posted up inside it and called the police, and waited.

Two came first, and stood in the open and talked. Then two more, and they split up into pairs and moved around, and Olly moved around
too, quietly, doing whatever he had to do to get them to separate. It took time. Eventually one of them went down a little side corridor
alone, her partner just on the other side of a wall, the two of them calling out to each other as they looked around. She had a flashlight
and a gun.

He came at her, and she shot him, point blank, the way anyone would, in that terror.

He didn't blink. He knew he'd been shot. It was like one of those mind-over-matter things where someone trains themselves not to feel what
their body is feeling. *I can ignore that. The Sheriff ignored that.* He took hold of her and fed.

Her partner found her later on the second floor, recovering, disoriented. Somewhere else, Olly dug the bullet out of himself. A little
discomfort. He felt fine.

It gave him an inflated sense of his own invulnerability. He did not know that it might not work like that next time.

He put his ear to the ground after that, and heard about a disturbance two nights after the thing with the Sheriff. The rumours were
inconsistent to the point of incoherence. But something had come to St. Joseph's, where the chantry was, where he'd gone up against the
Sheriff: some kind of nightmarish creature that had come through and terrorized the place.

Maybe those other guys had heard something.

He didn't know where exactly they'd be, only the general area. So one night he went to the park and sat on a bench and was conspicuous
and killed time until one of them noticed.

"Mr. Olly," said Ralph. "What brings you around?"

"Ralph. How are the new digs?"

"Wonderful."

"I heard about something. You guys seem pretty plugged in, especially in this area. I heard some sort of monster came through. Something.
Around the school. St. Joseph's."

"No. What kind of monster?"

"A few nights ago. Last night, maybe." He tried to remember how it had been put to him. "Something menacing and strange. Conflicting
descriptions. Some sort of huge body-horror thing. Real nasty. Cronenberg. People saw it."

"You'd know if you'd heard about it, I guess," said Ralph. "Must not have made much of a disturbance. Regardless of how disturbing it was."

"Maybe Ethan knows something. Is he around?"

"He usually is. You can find him if you look."

Ethan had called Rose earlier and not got through. What came back was a text: *Occupied. Stay where you are.* He'd answered with a
thumbs-up.

Now his phone buzzed again. Rose.

*Are you in the area? I need to come pick you up.*

He answered right away.
