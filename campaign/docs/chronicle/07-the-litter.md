## Shit Rolls Downhill

This is what they knew, by the time the year turned.

They had lent the bone to the Tremere, and the Tremere had lost it. The chantry had been boarded and guarded
and warded, and something had come through it anyway, as if none of that was there: something past anything
they understood about their own condition, something that turned people into door mantels. Dominik's ghoul
had been made into something else, and it had heard some kind of song. There had been water on the
carpet afterward, hot, and it wasn't anything chemical, and it wasn't only water. The bone was said to have
something to do with the Antediluvians, whatever those were exactly. And now the people who had lost it
expected the coterie to get it back, and the territory they had only just been given hung on whether they
could.

"Isn't that what the powerful do," said Ralph. "Make their problems your problems. It's really an act of
generosity."

"It's another way of saying shit rolls downhill," said Olly.

Ethan was moving his business into the new building, which meant paperwork. Incorporation, permits, the
build-out. It didn't happen in a night. Ralph had the apartment, a few blocks away. Olly had his notes on
fleshcrafting, and a monster he wanted to know more about, and Trieste, who wanted to get into the chantry and
get Malik out.

Then Trieste texted him. King Dre wasn't interested in helping. She didn't seem inclined to try anything else
about Malik. She and the gang, though, had *hit the jackpot*. He should bring his friends by some time.

*Sure*, he wrote back. *If you really hit it off with those guys, you want me to bring them?*

*I think they'd be very interested. Don't worry, we'll meet at night.* And, when he pushed: *Many hands make
light work.* Nothing else.

He knew how it would look. He'd met these two on a fool's errand for Trieste, and they obviously thought she
was crazy, and now he had to drag them to something else of hers. It was like babysitting somebody who's
very drunk at a party: *please don't let this reflect too badly on me*. But he was more or less her dog. So
he went to the park, and found them, and made the pitch.

"You remember my friend Trieste. She's recently stumbled into something I think could be really useful for
the investigation. I don't really know what it is. But she knows I'm busy with this, so it must be relevant.
If she says it's urgent, we should check it out. She wants us to swing by tonight."

"The girl who likes to look at the sun?" said Ralph.

"This is more of a two in the morning thing. We're not going to see the sun. It won't come up."

"I mean, that's insane," said Ethan. "But if she's survived it this long, she must know something."

## The Pit

The address was in the Distillery District, down by the lake, or as near the lake as the expressway let
you get. Forty blocks or so from the park: a short drive in the middle of the night, a long walk. Ralph drove.

On the way they compared what they had.

Ralph had two leads on the bone. One was Jason Cho, who'd offered him five million for it, and that was a dead
end; he knew it was a dead end, and brought it up anyway. The other was the two vampires in the alley. The one
who'd been doing the feeding had dropped the bone. Ralph had seen their faces. Then they'd disappeared.

"Vampire feeding on vampire," Ralph said. "Diablerie. I didn't know that was a thing."

Olly had known one thing about vampires feeding on vampires, which was that Rami sometimes fed on him, and that
was nothing like what Ralph was describing. What Ralph was describing sounded like what had been done to the husk
in the drawer.

So he told them about the body. The markings. He had pictures of the markings on his phone. He didn't have any
of the face; it hadn't seemed important.

"Is the body still around?"

"I had to break into a morgue. Maybe it's still there."

Ethan's question was simpler. Who had the horsepower to roll through a place like the chantry the way that thing
had? It seemed like it would be really hard. That had to narrow the list.

---

The Distillery District was distilleries, and light manufacturing, and alleys. They walked down one toward the
water, and ahead of them against the glow of the highway were three or four figures with shovels, standing
around the kind of heap a truck dumps on a lot to be picked up later. A couple of hundred feet away. The figures
had seen the car's lights come and go.

"Hey!" That was Lil Sis. "Come over here. We could use an extra shoveller."

It wasn't a heap. It was a grave, a makeshift one, and there were nine bodies in it.

"We bit off a little more than we could chew," said Trieste. One end was half filled in already. She nodded at
the other. "Go ahead and finish bleeding out those last couple. They're dead, but they need to be bled. And then
give them a little vitae. So they can have a rough wake-up."

Ralph had been reading. Anything about the occult he could get his hands on, including anything that might say
what he was. And he remembered this: an idea some of them had about the Embrace. Everywhere else in Kindred
society it was a thing you did rarely and carefully. You were an apex predator. You made one, or two, and you
invested everything in them, and you held on to power and structure. But there were some who believed the
opposite. Make them like fish lay eggs. Spam it. Whoever survives was meant to. He had never known whether that
was anything more than a theory.

It was more than a theory.

Olly remembered something too. Trieste had said, a long time ago, that she'd woken up in a pit with her crew.
And that the people who killed her hadn't made it. She had never said what she meant by that. Olly had assumed
they woke up hungry and killed the people who'd made them. Her crew, he thought, might include Lil Sis. Might have
included Malik.

He found a moment to tell the other two, quietly. There might be some danger when these ones woke up.

"No kidding," said Ralph. "I remember what I was like when I first woke up."

Olly counted. Trieste, Lil Sis, and Zuzu. Three of them, and three of the coterie. And nine in the pit.

---

The last three bodies were very recently dead. Not yet drained.

Something like that almost never happens. You can drink until the Hunger is simply gone, and nobody dies of it,
because nobody is left to die. Ethan drank until he was full. So did Olly.
Ralph abstained.

The second part, the vitae, none of them rushed to. Ethan knew more than the other two about what it meant. In
the Camarilla's city, making one of them without the Prince's permission was a capital crime. These people were
very clearly not operating within Camarilla guidelines. He met Ralph's eyes, and Olly's.

Olly went over to Trieste instead. "So. Are we having kids? What's going on here?"

"Whichever ones survive the sun," she said, "replenish our pack."

"Because you've been losing so many to standing out in the morning."

"There's been a gradual decline. But here's the thing. Did you know I stayed outside until eleven in the morning
the other week?"

"On the rooftop?"

"We found a way. You know how we snorted Jonah's ashes?"

"I couldn't forget it."

"We saved some. We've been experimenting. If you cut it the right way, it really helps with the sunburn. So this
is win-win. We get a few new recruits, and-or we get some raw material."

"Oh my God," said Olly. "Where did you find these people?"

"Zuzu found most of them, honestly. I think they were here for a bachelorette party." She shrugged. "It's fine.
They're from Saskatchewan."

"You said this is how you woke up. In a ditch."

"Yeah. There were like fifteen of us."

"And the people who put you in that ditch?"

"No idea. I didn't see them."

Ralph had been hearing fragments of this. Nobody was trying to keep it from him. "I think we should make
ourselves scarce," he said. "This isn't a scene we want to be involved in."

"Why'd you want me to bring these guys?" Olly asked Trieste.

"I thought they might be interested. Either in the sire part, or in getting some of the goods." She held out her
hand. Three vials of what looked like ash. "These are for you guys. Call it paying you back. Paying it forward.
Whatever."

"Three of them. Wow. I appreciate it."

She had one more, a bigger stoppered beaker, and she tapped it against his like glasses. "To Jonah."

"Are you doing it now?"

"No. I'm assuming we won't be together when you partake. So. In anticipation."

"Always the romantic." He looked at the pit. "Well. I guess I should give blood to one of these kiddos and see
what happens."

He cut himself and did it, for one of them. Ethan and Ralph didn't.

"This is psycho shit," said Ralph.

"I think I'm going to stick around," Olly said. "See what happens. You don't see this every day."

Ethan was doing math. The risks. The benefits.

"We could probably use their help," said Olly, "if we're going to take down whatever broke out of that chantry."

"We're trying to earn good graces with the establishment," said Ralph. "Involvement in this sort of thing is
counter-indicated."

"And what's the establishment ever done for you?" said Olly.

"They literally just gave us feeding grounds. Territory that could be ours."

"They also lost the valuable thing you loaned them."

"Which makes us valuable," said Ralph. "Makes us useful."

"All right," said Olly. "Fair enough. You were never here."

## Sunup

Trieste and Lil Sis saw to the last of them, and they all filled in the grave. Then they sat back and somebody lit
a joint. It was that kind of conversation, after: old children's cartoons, the shitty jobs they'd had when they
were alive.

Olly was comfortable. That surprised him a little. The sun-watching had alarmed him; it had looked borderline
suicidal. But making more of themselves to replace the ones they'd lost made sense. He didn't mind that it was
illegal. It seemed like a good hang.

Around four, he heard stirring in the dirt, and then there were hands coming up out of it.

He remembered his own first waking. Nightmarish. Not knowing where you were, and a wanting in you rolling stronger
than anything you'd felt alive. Rami had been there. Rami had turned it somewhere else fast. But the first instant
of it had been intense.

These ones had nobody to turn it for them. It was pure feral energy. They came out of the ground clawing, over one
another, in whatever they'd died in, going for anything. The first to find its feet took Lil Sis's shovel full in the
head, swung like a baseball bat, and went down. Zuzu picked up another shovel and waited.

Olly had not expected random violence against the vampire babies. He backed off.

"You want to find victims for them," Zuzu told him, matter-of-fact. "But first you've got to keep them in place long
enough to see if they have stamina. It's going to be a long hour or so till the sun comes up." She offered something.
"If you want to snort a little Jonah now, it'll buy us till seven or eight at least. More time than we'll need."

"I guess I'd better hit that. Aren't they going to be pissed when they finally get out, after you knock them in the
head all morning?"

"It wears off surprisingly quickly," said Trieste. "Once they're feeding." Then, as if it needed saying: "It's not that
the Masquerade they always talk so big about is sacred. But we have some self-preservation instinct. We're not completely
suicidal. Nine fresh ravenous ones charging off onto the highway paints a target on our backs. So we funnel them where we
want them. The plant over there opens in twenty minutes anyway. There'll be fresh blood, easy."

"Have you done this before?"

"We've done this before."

"And what happened to them?"

Zuzu shrugged. "I turned out fine. We lost most of my litter. Tragic." Another shrug. "They weren't cut out for it."

What followed was an hour of batting them back, half-heartedly. They were confused, and disoriented, and they fell over one
another, and then one would find its footing and charge, and get a shovel in the face. Some of them got hurt in ways that
would heal, roughly. Somebody went over to the plant and came back with a shift worker, unconscious, and held him ready, but
didn't throw him in.

Everyone took a little snort.

The sun came up.

All of them but one went to ash. Lil Sis tossed the shift worker to the one who was left, and it latched onto his neck and
fed in a crazed stupor, while the rest of them went about carefully gathering up what was left of the other eight.

Trieste and Lil Sis were contemporaries, Olly thought, watching them work. Both older than Zuzu. He helped where he could. He
tried to be a good sport about it, and they gave him credit for that.

"I'll let you know when the next batch is available," Trieste said. "And if you guys want to explore the sunlight, there's some
great stuff that's open during the day."

It had to be processed, she said, before it did what it did. A composite.

"Can I get a ride home? The guys I came with dipped."

They didn't have a ride. There was one of those shitty electric scooters. Olly rode it home along the highway in full daylight,
on borrowed time, with a nostril full of Jonah.

## Who Could Do That

Ethan was not going to crack a book. He was mostly literate. What he did was find people who knew things, starting with Rose, and
ask them who in this city was powerful enough to go through the chantry like that.

A handful, it turned out.

Evelyn Skye, the Baron of Kensington, who was Rose's own sire. Father Vivek, out in Riverdale, way northeast. King Dre, in
Scarborough. Someone named Raya, who claimed great powers without anyone having seen her demonstrate them: a Noddist priestess.
Ethan had heard the phrase in passing and paid it no mind. There was apparently a church of Caine, a vampire church, and she was
the local chapter's priestess. Elijah Kane, the Sheriff, who had shrugged off a car and a bullet in the space of a minute; he'd
qualify. And the Seneschal, Maeve, who was known to be powerful and rumoured to be much more powerful than she let on.

It was a longer list than Ethan would have guessed. And not one of them fit the description of what had been seen.

---

Ralph set up a drawing pad.

He remembered very little, personally. Something had gone into his head in that alley and pushed; there might be holes in what
he had. So he didn't try to remember. He did a couple of practice runs with his eyes open, to get the feel of the pad's edges. Then
he closed his eyes, and ran what he had over and over in his mind, a few frames, almost stills, of the tall one turning toward him,
and let it go straight down into his hand without trying to force it.

What came out was humanoid, which was something: it wasn't just lines. No hair, or else the hair pulled back tight. Hands that were
clawed, and not human hands. Ears that were not like an elf's but like a goblin's, some deformation of the body that he hadn't
consciously known he had seen. It looked alien. Monstrous before it looked like anything else. Whatever it had been born, the drawing
couldn't tell you: not a man or a woman, not from anywhere. A monstrous human first and foremost.

It looked more like his sire than like any other Kindred he'd met. It wasn't his sire.

---

Olly sent proof of life to the group chat. *Hey guys, that was a lot weirder than I expected. But I did get this powder that can help
with sun immunity, so, happy to share. We should still connect soon about the monster that's loose in your neighbourhood. Again, sorry
about that. I really didn't know what we were walking into.*

Ralph wrote back. *Can you take me to where you found that body you mentioned?*

## The Morgue, Again

Olly texted Claude first, to find out if the body was supposed to still be there. Claude didn't text back.

"Well. I didn't get word. Do you want to go check it out? We'll have to sneak into the morgue."

"I'm very good at breaking and entering," said Ralph. "It's like my second specialty."

They went that night. Olly knew the way and went quiet, a quiet so complete that his hands made no sound on anything they touched, and
Ralph did his own version, and between them they eased doors open and waited out the people still moving around a hospital in the small
hours, and came down to the drawer.

It stuck. Not locked, just jammed. They wrenched it open. The body was there, as Olly had left it: a withered husk of one of them, with
the inscriptions, and the holes and ports in the skin where things had gone in.

Ralph looked at it for a while. He was nearly certain. This was the one he'd seen pinned to the fence.

"We should take the body with us," Ralph said. "For Masquerade reasons. I don't want the FBI getting involved. Or whoever it is up
here. Sorry. I just came back from California."

"I think it's secure here. I know somebody who works here."

"Are they in the know enough to cover it up?"

"Is that really worth it to you? How would we even get it out?"

"You've got the feet. I grab the shoulders, and we walk."

"I've never smuggled a body out of anywhere."

"You seemed pretty comfortable with bodies yesterday."

"I was surprised. It was the first time." Olly considered the drawer. "First time for everything. All right. Let's get this guy out of
here."

There was a body bag. Without any water in it the husk weighed maybe forty pounds, which was one mercy; they didn't need a gurney or lab
coats or anybody to talk to. They carried it out the way they had come in. There would be questions later, at the hospital: who were
those people, why was that drawer wrenched open, why was it empty, what had even been in it, and why didn't the logs agree. None of
those questions would have anybody to answer them.

They put it in Ralph's trunk.

In the clear, Olly explained to Ralph what an athanor corporis was. The body in the trunk had been one, in its unlife: a thing for
storing blood concoctions, making them. "Like a vial," he said. "A Kindred vial. For drugs, basically."

He didn't say what else he knew about it.

"That's why I want to get in good with the establishment," said Ralph. He didn't say what he didn't want to end up as.
It was in his trunk.

"I'd hate that," said Olly.

## The Marx Brothers

Ethan was at the office, doing admin, when Ralph came in with Olly behind him.

"Ethan, my man. How's it going? I've got a corpse in the car." Ralph smiled. "I've got a plan. It's stupid. But I think it'd be
good."

"You're really instilling confidence."

"It's better than I'm selling it." It was simple. They had the victim of their prime suspect. They had a drawing of the suspect. They
should go to the chantry and give Dominik an update on his little quest. Maybe he'd recognize the picture. Maybe the magic vampires
could do something with a victim's body to track the one who'd done it. Or maybe they'd just be glad somebody had got a Kindred corpse
out of human hands. "Either way it makes us look good. It looks like we're making progress. It buys us time. We keep our turf a little
longer."

"Demonstrable progress," said Ethan. "All right."

He texted Rose. *We found this. We think it might be related. We were also doing a little Masquerade protection, as a bonus. We're
thinking of going over to the chantry to give them the update. What do you think?*

The dots came up. Went away. Came back. Either she was writing a novel or she was writing and deleting, writing and deleting. What
came, eventually, was short.

*I've called ahead to let them know you're coming.*

"Inspiring," said Ralph.

---

The iron door had been repaired. You'd never know. If you looked very closely there were signs of recent work, but the whole thing had
been cleaned and put right in a matter of days, and that told you something: somebody had paid a great deal of money to have very, very
competent people drop whatever they were doing and do this first.

They left the body in the back seat of Ralph's car under a jacket, and knocked.

Dominik opened the door himself, and looked at them with contempt. "Why are you here?"

"Two things," said Ralph. "A status update on our investigation, and on recovering the relic. And a semi-related matter. A body we've
recovered."

Dominik didn't move out of the doorway. He waited.

Ralph held up the printout. "I believe this is our prime suspect."

Dominik looked at it. "I don't recognize them. Am I supposed to?"

"I assumed you know many more people than I do. And these are very distinctive features. They might indicate a particular origin."

Dominik's face said, very clearly, that his time was being wasted.

"The second thing. I have the body of another vampire. One this person diablerized. It was in a hospital morgue."

"You have it here?"

"Yes."

"Bring it in."

He went inside, and they could hear him talking to people, having things moved. All three of them went down to the car together and all
three came back up together. Nobody wanted to be the one left alone with Dominik.

When they came back there was a large table with a heavy cloth over it, and plastic under the cloth, and instruments laid out, some of them
surgical and some of them ceremonial, a great many little blades and shears. They brought the bag all the way in. Dominik opened it, and his
whole manner changed.

"How did you find this?"

"Through disreputable thin-blood contacts," said Ralph.

"Well. Occasionally you seem to have some worth."

He went over the body with his hands, and they could feel him getting frustrated. He went into another room and came back with needles, and
tried to draw something out of it, anything, and came up dry. He started asking questions and stopping halfway through them, deciding, it
seemed, both whether they were worth asking and how much he wanted them to know. Were there fluids near the body? Was this everything that was
there when it was found? What had their contact said?

"I don't know where the body was recovered from," said Ralph. "When I saw him diablerize this one, he wiped my memory, briefly, and dropped the
bone. He took the body with him. It turned up somewhere else."

"And you don't know what he had—" Dominik stopped. Started again. "Do any of you have connections to a blood alchemist?"

"Yes," said Ralph, who didn't, but was sure somebody did.

"There are various markings here. It's possible there's some kind of warding ritual inscribed upon the skin. But I suspect this one was involved
in some of the more *disreputable*, as you say, alchemical concoctions we have been dealing with. If you could provide a referral. Ideally
someone willing to come here and examine it under my observation. We could at least rule some things out."

"I'll see what we can do."

Dominik was turning the head side to side. "I didn't realize," he said, mostly to himself, "that we had local Tzimisce."

"Can I ask what indicates Tzimisce?" said Olly.

Dominik looked at him the way you'd explain something to a very small child. He turned the head for them. There was a build-up of skin and
muscle that was simply not human: whole additional muscles. He drew the skin down at the collarbone, and there were ridges there, growing out
of the bone. Not human. Not even what the Nosferatu tended to have.

"Protrusions like these are usually indicative of someone molding their own body into some other shape. It isn't unique to the Tzimisce. But
it is very rarely seen outside them."

"So this Kindred was morphing himself."

"That would be my suspicion."

"Your ghoul got fleshcrafted," said Olly. "And the Tzimisce are commonly associated with fleshcrafting. That's kind of an interesting
coincidence."

"I don't believe in coincidences."

"What would be the political dimensions? If there was a small group of Tzimisce in Toronto we didn't know about?"

"In principle, no matter. They are under the Prince's authority as any others. Historically, they have the unfortunate tendency of aligning
with the Sabbat. And of disregarding the Masquerade and our traditions in their entirety. If they were to swear fealty, it would be no matter."
He looked down at the husk. "But what happened to my ghoul strongly indicates another. Clearly not this one. Perhaps its sire. Very
interesting." He straightened. "Unfortunately it is fully drained of any vitae that might have identified it. However. If your courtroom sketch
is in fact the diablerist, we could determine some *very* interesting things through an examination of their vitae. We wouldn't even need to
kill them right away."

"I appreciate that," said Ralph.

"I know I'll enjoy it. Anyway. Be on your way."

"We'll report again when we have substantive progress."

"Great to see you, Dominik!" said Olly.

Dominik did not respond to that.

---

It had gone better than it had any right to. They'd asked him things and he'd answered, and they'd asked follow-ups, and he'd answered those
too. Olly had a theory about why. The body had changed what they were worth to him, a little. What it had done to his curiosity was a lot more
than a little. And the Tzimisce part had surprised him; he'd been thinking it through out loud.

And someone who seemed so deep in blood magic was looking outside for help with alchemy. That was interesting too. Maybe it was a corner of
things he knew less about.

"My sire's a blood alchemist," Olly said, "if that's any use."

"I'm sure only thin-bloods know any," said Ralph. "Rose probably knows a couple. If she doesn't, we'll find one." He was pleased. "I think we've
gone from the Three Stooges to the Marx Brothers, in his eyes."

"That's a promotion. Which one's Groucho?"

"Me. Of course. I'm the leader."

The part Olly kept turning over was different. They'd been thinking the one who killed this Kindred was the one who went through the chantry.
Now it looked like it was someone who'd been *close* to the victim. A sire, maybe.

"So it's a one-Tzimisce problem," said Ralph. "Or a three-Tzimisce problem."

## Rami

Ralph kept both places. Most nights he slept at Bill's. The apartment was a show apartment, for bringing people back to. Seductive feeding.
Meetings with vampires. That kind of thing.

"Those shovel-head people," he said to Olly, at some point. "A bachelorette party, in its entirety. Not good people. And I'm not squeamish.
Someone has to die to become a vampire. Someone dies to feed us, or gets caught in the crossfire. That's the cost of things. But that seemed
excessive. Wasteful."

"I'm sure they were being very douchey," said Olly.

The three of them went over the park, their ground, together. There was something under it: a line of something running through the earth, a
current, that each of them felt in their own way. Nobody gives up prime ground. Maybe it had been given to them for a reason.

---

Rami, when Olly came, didn't let him talk right away. He came close, and took Olly by the back of the neck, and fed. Not long. A top-off. Olly
felt himself get hungrier.

"Okay," said Rami. "I can concentrate now. What's going on?"

"You've been eagerly waiting for an update on the body. And I've stumbled into a lot of information. I know somebody who witnessed the murder of
that corpse. It definitely was an athanor. And we took it to Dominik, at the chantry. He had a closer look, and he thought a blood alchemist
might be able to give a better assessment of what happened to it. So I thought of you. I didn't mention your name."

"I'm sorry. You went to Dominik. At the *chantry*."

"Do you remember hearing about this abomination downtown, wreaking havoc?"

"I don't keep up with the news."

"I'm looking into it for him."

"Sit down."

He pointed at his work chair, and started hooking Olly up, and ran him through a cycle: flushing the vitae out of him, straining something out of
it, putting something else in, and flushing it back. When it was over Olly's blood ran sanguine. What was in him now, Rami said, was much less
valuable, more neutral. There was no hurry for it.

Olly understood, slowly, what had just happened. What had been cooking in him had been worth a great deal. When Rami heard *Dominik*, the first
thing he'd thought was how much Dominik would love to have it. And he had panicked.

"Do you think Kindred can parse what's inside me? From interacting with me?"

"I don't know of a means to do that. I don't know what some of these blood-magic powers can and can't do. I'd be surprised if they could tell
the qualities of what you had brewing at a given time. But they don't need to, if they know that you are an athanor. All they need to do is tap
you, and experiment."

"But I'm not walking around with a sign on my back. There's no way to tell by looking at me."

"Not looking at you as you are, no. But again. I don't know what means he has." Rami sat back. "I think we have been extraordinarily lucky."

"Have you met this guy? He's imposing."

"I have avoided meeting him."

"Do you want to meet him? A boon from him is worth a lot."

"I will meet him once," said Rami. "So. Let me know what I need to bring, and when. And I will be there."

What Rami did not need to say was that Dominik must never know he was Olly's sire. Olly was thin-blooded, and unregistered, and he had been made
without anyone's permission. Rami was not thin-blooded. But he did not like being governed. He had avoided the Barons, and avoided the Camarilla,
and done his own thing in private. Even in the blood-alchemy scene he kept to himself, which was part of why he had never come up on Rose's radar as a
problem. He had enormous contempt for the ones who played around irresponsibly, or who got addicted to strange things. He had standards and he kept
to them, in his lane, and paid no attention to what anyone did on either side of it. It wasn't, Olly thought, a very coherent worldview.

## Distillation

Rose came by to see the new place, and Ethan showed her around. The business setup. The holding cells going in, over here. And somewhere in the
tour he slipped in the body, and Dominik, and how it had gone, because keeping her up to date was what he did.

"That's partly why I came by," said Rose. "He's been very active, in some of the networks I'm active in. Looking for some very specific things.
You and I have confiscated a fair amount of paraphernalia over the last seven or eight months. Some of it might be very useful to him. I didn't want
to duplicate efforts. I want maximum leverage. How much competition do you think we have? Can we be his exclusive supplier, on the alchemy side?"

Olly jumped like he'd put his hand on a hot stove.

"That's just crazy that you bring that up," he said, "because that body we found was one. You know."

What he said was straight-faced enough, and plausible enough, that Ralph mentally checked the box. *Yep. Checks out.*

Ethan was watching his eyes, and saw sheer panic in them.

It told him one of two things. Either Olly could do it himself. Or Olly knew somebody who could, and did not want that discovered at all: somebody
he was very close to, or somebody very valuable, held very close to the chest. Ethan didn't know Olly well enough to know which.

They set it up the only way they could. Rose would talk to Dominik. Dominik would name a night. And Rami would come to the chantry, once.
