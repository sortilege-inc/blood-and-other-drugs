## The Drawer

The morgue was the kind of place Olly was good at.

Not the medicine. He didn't know the first thing about the medicine. But a building is a building,
and every building has the way it wants you to walk through it and the way you actually can. He could
have put on a hi-vis vest and carried a clipboard. That works almost anywhere. Instead he went the
long way, peering round corners, and twice he came round one and found a whole hallway he hadn't
known was there, that anyone could have come down while he stood in it. He filed those away for the
trip out.

Claude had a room with a separate key. Rami had told him which drawer.

The body inside was naked. Not rotted. Not old. Dried, somehow, like something that had been emptied
and left in a warm place: a husk of what it had been, without the years that ought to go with that.
There were tattoos on it. There were scars that weren't accidents, patterns cut on purpose.

Olly didn't know what a coroner would see. He knew what *he* saw, because he'd lived it for a year and
a half. The marks where things had gone in. The places the skin had been worked. This one had been a
furnace. An athanor, like him: something cooking inside it, distilling, and from the way it had dried
out, whatever it had been cooking wasn't in there anymore.

Someone had drained it. All of it.

He looked at the tattoos a long time. He knew some things about the ink, the way anyone in the scene
did. Thin-bloods caught on the Camarilla's ground without somebody to answer for them tended not to
leave it. There were only two ways around that. Walk in behind someone with enough weight to tell
the killers to fuck off, or be *registered*: branded, permanently, so the court knew who you were and
who you belonged to, and extended you the courtesy of not destroying you just for existing.

Some of the markings on the body reminded him of that. But there was no brand. These were something
else, and he didn't know what.

He knew who might. Darya did ink. Blood-alchemy ink, the only kind that holds in dead skin, and for the
people who didn't want to commit to the brand and everything that came with it, she did something that
passed for one. And she'd be at the COAG this week. The COAG was a pop-up, the Kindred equivalent of
one of Olly's own parties: a location that moved, and if you knew, you knew. This week it was in Lower
Queen station, the one under Queen Street that had been sealed up since the sixties. Right in the
middle of Camarilla ground.

He took pictures of every mark. He always had his camera.

---

"Besides you," Rami said, looking at the photographs, "I know of no other in this entire city. And
that one was not one I knew of."

"If this guy had a target on his back," Olly said, "I wonder if I've had one this whole time. I don't
know how many people know about our arrangement."

"Let me put it this way. The kind of people who know would far rather have you working. You are much
more valuable in your function than as a corpse."

"I see," Olly said.

He did not see. Something itched at the back of his brain and didn't stop. Rami had never, in a year
and a half, cared how Olly *felt* about anything. It wasn't like him to start now.

## The Bail Bonds Office

Ethan's office was not in a good part of town, which was the point.

A regular office from the front. Behind the glass door, a second door of bars. Roll-down steel shutters
on the windows. A holding cell with a bunk and a toilet and nothing else, and a heavy door on a room full
of body armour and weapons that were only mostly not lethal. Cameras on everything, including the cell.
Break in, and you'd get there eventually, but everyone in the neighbourhood would hear you.

Ralph texted his roommate that he wasn't coming home. *Got a place to crash, no windows or anything.*

*Lucky you*, the roommate wrote back.

The cell door was unlocked. Ralph could have walked out any time. He'd also have been on camera the whole
way, and he knew that, and so did Ethan.

He woke the next night hungry and did what he always did when he woke up hungry and couldn't eat yet:
sit-ups. Push-ups. His whole routine, like the guy in *American Psycho*. None of it did anything for a
body like his anymore. The routine was the point.

Ethan found him on the floor of the cell, mid-set.

"Morning," Ethan said. "Evening. Whatever."

Rose had the bone now. Somewhere safe, somewhere neither of them had been told. Ralph trusted that as far
as he had to.

"That bone," Ethan said. "It's going to paint a target on your back. It already has, apparently. You need
friends. And you need friends with weight. I'm just being blunt with you."

"I know it's important," Ralph said. "I know the kind of people who want it don't fuck around."

"What kind of people do you think those are?"

"Old ones. Strong ones."

Ethan didn't know much more than that himself. The Baron of Kensington, Evelyn, was good to thin-bloods,
better than most. She was also strange, and deep into things Ethan kept a careful distance from: ghosts,
spells, the kind of thing he'd rather not have explained to him. Rose had made him, and Rose was showing
him the ropes, and he was, by his own reckoning, not the brightest crayon in the box. Trying to drink from
a fire hose wasn't going to work. So he took the bits and pieces she gave him and didn't push.

Rose never texted. She used envelopes, always the same kind, always to the same address, always the same
few words, just enough to take the next step. The one waiting tonight said she had *multiple interested
parties*. They should come to the COAG and meet them.

"Looks like there's a lot of interest," Ethan said.

"I wasn't looking to sell it," Ralph said. "But for the right price." He thought about it. "It'd be a chance
to find out more about it. If they're interested, they know more than I do. No offense."

"She knows more than I do. She's old." Ethan shrugged. "I can't speak for her."

"Why did she want you to bring me here? I figured it was some kind of trap. Apparently not, since I'm not
locked up."

"Safe location. We don't know where you keep it; you do. So nothing happens to you. The doors lock from
outside, but you can open them. I wouldn't go strolling around this neighbourhood at night. But I'm not
going to stop you."

Ralph had a night to himself before the COAG. He meant to spend some of it getting the bone.

"You want me to come?" Ethan said.

"No," Ralph said. "Obviously not. I'm trusting you a lot. Just not to the level of where I keep my stuff when
I don't want it found. No offense."

---

Before he went out, Ethan sat down and did what Rose had taught him.

He still had the fire in him, two deep burns that hadn't closed. The first time he'd done this she had
coached him through it, sitting there concentrating, putting his whole weight behind one idea, and it had
wrung him out like a half-marathon, left him ravenous. This time he just sat and applied what willpower he
had to the thought of not hurting anymore, and the burns closed, and it cost him less than he'd expected.
Experience, maybe.

He was still hungry. He was usually hungry.

## Three Suppers

Most of Ethan's clients couldn't make bail. That was sort of the business.

The calls came after the bars closed, two o'clock onward, when people got caught up in whatever they got
caught up in. A night guy took them and texted Ethan when there was *one for you*. It was understood around
the office, the way things are understood without anybody saying them, that if a sex worker got picked up and
couldn't pay the fee, Ethan would sometimes arrange an alternative. The staff assumed what they assumed. They
were wrong, and it suited him that they were wrong.

And a bail jumper who'd skipped his court date was a lot easier to bring in a couple of pints short.

The call that night came in around three. Ethan took it himself. He didn't take too much. He never did.

*I had the meal*, he thought, on the way back. *I just didn't have dessert.*

---

Ralph ate in his own neighbourhood, where he knew the houses.

He had standards. He wanted clean blood, no drugs, no diseases, which meant cars with *26.2* stickers on
the bumper, or the respectable old couple with the nice garden. He went out in his jogging clothes, made
himself quiet, let himself in, took a small drink from someone asleep, and left. He fed often so he never
had to take much.

This house he'd visited before. The last time, he'd found their wifi unsecured and downloaded the app that
ran the doorbell camera and the smart lock, and the house had simply let him have it. Tonight he turned off
the front camera from his phone, walked up the path, and the door opened for him.

---

Olly was on edge, and he knew why: the husk in the drawer. He wanted something that felt like judgement.

He texted Dmitri first, and waited, and Dmitri didn't answer. So he texted Trieste, and made it sound like
he didn't care either way. He never liked needing something from her.

She picked the place: an old church in a worn-down part of town, a little on the nose. When he came in she
was standing up behind the altar, in the dark, holding a knife.

"Shall I cut this one loose?"

"Play ball," said Olly.

Trieste cut two cords and a woman got up off the floor. Forties. Suit jacket and slacks, like she'd come
from an office. Gagged. She moved away from Trieste fast and saw Olly and moved sideways.

"Where'd you find this one?" Olly said.

"You know Ghislaine Maxwell?" Trieste put the knife away. "This one was a secretary for one of the Catholic
churches. Functions similarly. Thought you might enjoy it." She headed for the door. "We're doing another
sunrise in a few nights, if you want to come."

"You know I'll be there."

"Bring your friends."

Olly went up into the rafters. The woman ran one way, then another, and he tracked her from above and dropped
down in front of her, and she stopped dead and didn't scream and didn't faint. She just froze.

That was what he liked. Like the kind of lizard that won't eat anything that isn't moving: you let the
crickets out and close the doors and let it hunt. Olly wanted his prey running.

He didn't kill her. He left her on the steps of a police station, which seemed to him to say something. He
wasn't sure what, exactly. But he needed it to mean something, or he couldn't do it at all.

## Jason Cho

Ralph was getting ready for the day, back in his own basement, when the doorbell rang.

He checked the camera. A man in a good suit on the front step. The same man his roommate had described.

Ralph went up with a baseball bat and held it out of sight behind the door frame.

"Oh, violence won't be necessary," the man said, pleasantly, before Ralph had said a word. He had a tablet.
"The name's Jason Cho. I heard you may have something of interest. I'd be happy to buy it from you."

"What object would that be?"

"I think you know. Unless you have more than one."

"What are you offering?"

"We can start at five million."

Ralph didn't say anything for a moment.

"I see I've got your attention," said Jason Cho.

"Yes. Yes, you have. I knew it was valuable. I didn't know it was *that* valuable."

"It's all a matter of valuable to whom." Cho was open to suggestions about how to move that much. He'd done
blockchain. He'd done gold. He could do the transaction here and now, if Ralph had the item.

"It's in a safety deposit box," Ralph said. "At a bank. Because I knew it was valuable. Do you have an office?
We could meet tomorrow."

No office. Instead Cho held up the tablet with a QR code on it: scan it, request five million, sign the terms,
and he'd negotiate the transfer. Then he held out his hand.

Ralph shook it and paid attention. Warm. Breathing. Blinking. And under all of that, no pulse. Cho was exactly
as dead as Ralph was, and wearing it just as well.

Ralph took a photo of the QR code. He did not scan it. You don't scan a stranger's QR code. Nothing could
possibly go wrong with that.

---

The next evening his roommate was at the kitchen table, scrolling on his phone, and there was a business card
on the table beside him. *Jason Cho — Collector.* No number. No address. Nothing.

"Guy came by for you last night. Corporate type. Reporter or agent or something."

Ralph stuck the card on the fridge. "I found an antique. I've been trying to auction it off. It's worth more
than I thought. I'm meeting buyers tomorrow."

"Like a book?"

"It's an old bone. From Greece or something."

"What stops anyone from just carving an old bone?"

"It has to be a certain age. Experts can tell. Same reason you can't just paint a Picasso."

"There are forgers."

"There are. These people are sending lawyers around. They're serious."

"Well," said the roommate, "don't mix me up with the lawyers," and went to bed.

## Lower Queen

Ralph called the office when he woke. Ethan wasn't in yet. They offered to have him call back; Ralph said
don't bother, it's time-sensitive, he'll want to talk to me. Ethan called back the moment he woke.

"Long time no talk. I'm coming to the office. Something's come up. It might change plans."

He came in with a hard-shell Pelican case. He'd put a couple of road flares in it from the garage. He had the
bone under his jacket.

"I had a surprise visitor right before I went down for the day. You know a guy named, and I use the word *guy*
loosely, Jason Cho?"

Ethan didn't. The name didn't ring any bell at all. The number did. Ethan had once thought he was doing
amazing because he'd cleared a hundred grand in a year.

"Look," he said. "If you want to sell it, sell it. But that was his *first* offer. He came looking for you. You
might come out further ahead."

"With that kind of money," Ralph said, "I always worry the price difference between paying me and killing me
gets very, very small."

He still wanted someone watching his back, whoever he sold to. He'd spread the wealth for it. Cho wasn't alive,
he was pretty sure.

Ethan didn't know what Rose wanted, exactly. He knew she was a power player, or at least, from where he stood,
that she said things and then things got done, or people got hurt. As long as he took care of his boss, his boss
would take care of him. And what he'd been told was: keep the guy alive, or unalive, and keep the bone safe.
At least until the meeting.

They took the company car, an intentionally forgettable Honda Civic, a bit beat-up, the kind of car that blends
in better in a bad neighbourhood than a good one, and drove down toward Queen Street.

Two thin-bloods, one of them carrying a bone worth five million dollars, driving into the Prince's ground.

Nothing happened. That was almost worse. There was one moment, a car that might have been paying attention to
them and then wasn't, like passing a state trooper doing ten over, and nothing came of it. But by the time they
came down into the old station, Ethan's blood was up, and Ralph could feel it in himself too.

Rose was waiting, and she was irritable.

---

The COAG was not Elysium. Ethan had heard of Elysium, the Camarilla's neutral ground, with rules. This was lower
class than that and lower key, run on a gentleman's agreement. It looked, more than anything, like a small flea
market or a new-age fair that had been set up in a tunnel: some vendors with semi-private booths for doing real
business, and out in the open, crystals and love potions and occult bric-a-brac for the mortals who came for the
edge of it all. Some of those mortals were ghouls. Everybody was there for the strange. There was a lot of talking.
There were a lot of stories.

A hundred Kindred in Toronto, people said, at least, and probably too many. And more ghouls, and more hangers-on
than that.

Ralph didn't look at what was for sale. He looked at who was buying.

Most of them were marks: people with money who bought whatever had the right look and a good pitch. What interested
him were the outliers, and they were outliers in every direction. A man who looked homeless holding his own, as an
equal, in a conversation with a banker in a very good suit. Someone in scrubs trousers with a jacket thrown over them,
straight from a shift. Two kids who couldn't have been more than fourteen or fifteen, running their own stall.

And across the room, a sharp suit and pomaded hair, different suit than last night. Jason Cho. Moving through the
crowd like he was looking for somebody.

## The Mark

Rose was blunt with Ralph.

"Excuse me," she said, "but you seem exceptionally ignorant. Do you know what a Methuselah is?"

He didn't.

What she told him then, he only half followed: something about the Kindred who were much older, much stronger, who
had died a long time ago. What her sources said the bone had to do with that. If they were right, she said, it was worth
far more than money. She wanted to make some deals. She had buyers here who would pay dearly, but they'd need to know
it was real, and have the chance to possess it, if it came to that.

Ralph showed her Jason Cho's card.

Her face did something, a frown, quickly erased. She handed the card back.

"He approached you?"

"Yes. And made a very sizable offer."

"I believe it." She looked at him. "What do you need, out of this deal?"

"Two things, pulling different ways," Ralph said. "I need to learn about what I am, and what I'm being dragged into.
Not *dragged*. I'm doing this willingly. I'm looking forward to immortality. I need to know all of it. I need people I
can trust to watch my back, and whose backs I can watch, so I don't get squished by somebody older than me. And I need
money. I have expensive tastes. I like money."

Rose nodded, as if that was about what she'd expected.

To meet the buyers here, she said, the two of them needed a mark. There were people in this room who would not deal
with two thin-bloods walking around loose. The only way she could bring them in was if they carried something that said
they were hers, answerable to her, under her seat.

She showed them. A few sweeping lines, nothing fancy.

Once he'd seen it, Ralph started seeing it on other people around the room.

"A temporary tattoo," said Ethan. "Okay. Weird. Sure."

## Lil Sis

Olly was working the room in code.

He wasn't sure he needed to. Everybody here was a vampire; it wasn't like the cops were listening. But you buy drugs by
talking around them, and old habits were old habits. He drifted down the little side passages off the main tunnel and back
out, talking to people who looked like they might be into it, and down one of the nooks he found Todd.

"You okay, man? What happened after I saw you? Did you guys get out all right?"

"I'm just glad I got out with my head," Todd said. "I don't know what the fuck that was about. We're a complete bust. I'm
trying to go independent." He patted a suitcase. "I've got a stash, at least."

The suitcase was full of little bottles, and every one of them was a mood. One would get you drunk, and ran phlegmatic.
One was supposed to give you sleep with good dreams. Six or eight of them, all needing their particular kind of blood and
time to brew, and a furnace. Todd wanted cash, or opportunities with Rami.

"Rami doesn't party," Olly said. "He wants nothing to do with this stuff. I want to be where *you* are. On your level. I
can pay. Or I can do favours for you. We don't need to bring Rami into it."

Todd thought about it. "Does he have downtime? If we needed lab equipment?"

"Lab equipment," said Olly, "is not a problem for me."

Todd wrote down an address; they'd meet up after, and talk. Then he let Olly pick. Olly picked the melancholic one,
because it wasn't his usual.

"I call that one the Strength of Sadness," Todd said. "It's good for when you need your defences up. Emotionally and
otherwise."

He'd spotted where Darya was working. He was heading back out into the main tunnel when he stopped short.

Rose. The guy who'd caught fire. And a third one he didn't know. Walking together across the open floor.

The last time he'd seen those people he'd nearly been burned alive. He found somewhere else to be, quietly, where they
wouldn't notice him.

Somebody clapped him on the back.

"Hey! How you doing?" It was Lil Sis, from Trieste's crew. "Haven't seen you since the rooftop. How's your friend?"

"Oh, we're good. I wish I was out on the roof more often. Have you guys been out since?"

"Not since Jonah got crispy." She said it the way you'd say it rained. "That was sad, how that happened. But I guess that's
just how it goes. It's going to be all our turns sometime."

"Maybe," said Olly. "But if you didn't sit out on the roof waiting for the sun, it wouldn't have to be. I'd rather
choose my time a little. Not all the way. A little."

"Yeah," said Lil Sis. "I see what you're saying."

"It was great being out there with you guys, though. What are you here for?"

"Malik had some stuff to offload. We found a score. Couple of old books."

The way she told it, and the way she didn't, Olly understood where they'd come from: a Ravnos stash, somebody's cache of
treasures, cleaned out. Across the room, Ethan was listening too, and came to the same conclusion.

Somewhere around then the two of them clocked each other. Neither of them said so.

## The Frontrunner

Rose split off to her meetings: some for the bone, some her own. She'd be behind a curtain in one of the booths, close
enough to shout for.

Ethan and Ralph got in line for Darya.

"So you're a legit skip tracer," Ralph said. "Bounty hunter. Bail bondsman. I assume you can handle yourself if things get
critical?"

"Nothing too crazy," said Ethan. "But yeah. With humans. Maybe a couple more than one at a time. That's why my
eyes don't stay still."

Jason Cho came through the crowd in a straight line toward them.

"Hey," Ethan said, low. "You know that guy?"

"That's the guy."

Cho put his hand out to Ethan, warmly. "Jason Cho. And you are?"

"Ethan. Pleased to meet you."

"Likewise. Are you an associate of our friend here?"

"Yeah. We're partners."

"Well." Cho turned to Ralph. "I must say I'm sad not to have heard from you yet. Is there anything I can answer?"

"Not yet. You're still the frontrunner."

"Happy to hear it. If there's anything I can do to keep things smooth, just let me know. If you have a competing offer,
I'm happy to discuss. In all likelihood I'll check in with you before I leave."

Ethan went in first, with the case on his lap and Rose's voice somewhere on the other side of a curtain. He kept his ears
open for Ralph.

---

Olly went looking for Darya and found her assistant instead.

"I don't need ink today," he said. "But I had some questions, and I thought she'd be the person to answer them. Is there
any chance she's got a minute? I know she's busy. Everybody's here for her."

"I'll see if we can fit you in. It's a working night; she's filling all her blocks. But if somebody finishes early, hang
around. You might catch her for a few minutes between clients."

"That's all I need. I'll hang out."

So he was loitering outside Darya's booth when the man with the bone case, the one Ethan had been walking with, came and
stood next to him.

"You have a briefcase?" Olly said. The guy didn't; he'd handed it off.

"Ethan's got it. Ethan's the one getting tattooed."

"Oh. Right." Olly looked at the curtain. "He looks better than last time. A little less crispy. Those guys you're with,
friends of yours?"

"Maybe. New friends." The man put out a hand. "Ralph."

"Olly. It's always good to make new friends. Also good to know who you're running with." He dropped his voice a little.
"I ran into those guys at an operation about a week ago. Kind of a domineering vibe. Things got violent. I'm not saying it
was on them. But I wonder how you got wrapped up in what they're doing."

"We have mutual business interests."

"Well. I hope you're on their side of that business."

"What's your business?"

"Me? I throw parties. I move things around. I know people, I know places." Olly waved at the tunnel. "We had a party right
here, a few months back. Huge. I think I saw you?"

"No. I only came back from California a couple of months ago."

"Oh. You've just got one of those faces."

"One of those astoundingly beautiful faces."

"Words out of my mouth," said Olly.

Ethan came out. Ralph went in. Ethan took up a post outside the curtain like a club bouncer, watching everybody who came
near, listening for any noise out of Ralph at all.

The guy with the phone, leaning the other way and scrolling, didn't look like a threat. He didn't look like anything. But
about five minutes in, Ethan noticed he wouldn't make eye contact, and kept not making it, and slowly Ethan remembered why.

He walked over. "I know you, don't I?"

"A lot of people know me around here. You do look familiar."

"No. I remember you. You were in that basement."

"Oh my God," said Olly, as if it had only just come back to him. "That guy burned you."

"Yeah," said Ethan. "That fucking hurt."

"You okay? You're in pretty good shape for what happened."

"Mostly okay. Yeah."

"Glad to hear it. That was not good."

"Was that a friend of yours? The flamethrower guy?"

"No. I'm a peaceful guy, really. I wouldn't get wrapped up in something like that."

Everything about the truth, Olly thought, was the wrong thing to lead with. That he'd been stoned out of his mind the
whole time, for one. That those were his people, sort of, whose stuff these guys had been stealing. So he gave Ethan the
version where he was just a delivery guy waiting for a signature, wrong place, wrong time, and he gave it well.

"Sometimes I run packages," he said. "Pay the vampire bills. You know how it is. I just had a job. I was about to get out
of there, and then things went down. Sorry I couldn't have done more. Honestly."

Ethan heard all of it. He also heard what was under it: a nervous, paranoid *please don't poke at this*. And he
remembered a gun, down at this guy's side.

*Then why'd you have a gun in your hand?* He didn't say it.

"All right," he said instead. "So you were just there. Wrong place, wrong time. That's what you're saying."

## The Sheriff

Something at the edge of Ethan's eye made him look up.

A man, very tall, six foot plenty, in a suit that was for working in, not for looking at. He was standing over two
smaller figures, and it was about to turn into something. The two smaller figures were the ones Olly had been talking to.

Ethan knew who the tall man was. He'd been told who to tip his hat to in this city, and who not to cross at all. That
was Elijah Kane. The Sheriff.

"Looks like your friends are in some shit," Ethan said.

"Oh, shit." Olly looked. "I'm sure it's a misunderstanding."

"Misunderstandings follow you around. Is that how that works?"

"I'm so misunderstood."

Olly edged closer, trying to hear. He couldn't make out what the Sheriff's problem was. He could only hear how hot it
was running.

Then he did the stupidest thing he'd done all year, which was a competitive category. He walked straight into it.

"What seems to be the problem here?" he said. "Officer?"

The Sheriff turned and looked down at him. "These piece-of-shit thin-bloods yours?"

"They're friends of mine. Good folks. Seems like there must have been some kind of confusion, if you've got a problem
with them."

"And your name is?"

"Todd," said Olly.

"Todd. And your sire?"

This was the part where honesty would have been a mistake. You didn't just make one of them in this city; you asked the
Prince first, and Olly was nearly sure Rami never had. So he named a different Banu Haqim, one he'd met once and hadn't
liked, and put everything he had into saying it like it was true.

The Sheriff looked at him for a long moment. His face screwed up, like a man tasting something he couldn't place.

"Take care of it myself," he said.

He took out zip ties. He bound Lil Sis's hands behind her, then Malik's. Then he picked them up, one in each hand, like
luggage.

"I'll bring you to Tash myself."

He walked away with them through the crowd. Olly didn't know who Tash was. He did notice, standing there with his hands
free, that he was not the third thing being carried.

---

Ralph came out of Darya's booth with fresh lines on his skin in time to see the end of it.

"Is that something that's supposed to happen?"

"That was the Sheriff," Ethan said. "Far as I understand it, as long as he's doing what the Prince wants, he can do
whatever he wants."

"That Prince word again. I assume *Sheriff* is another one of those capitalized words."

"There's a few of them," said Ethan. "That's a big one."

Rose came back. She was pleased they both had their marks. She smiled.

"The real one," she said, "is going to be Dominik Levesque. Tremere. Very influential, and a serious scholar of the occult.
He's the one I'm hoping works out. The meeting I just came out of is our contingency. And leverage. We have options. This
is great."

Ralph asked what Jason Cho was likely to do, if he was disappointed.

Rose thought about it. "He's not buying for himself. Somebody sent him. The question is who. Until we know that, I can't
tell you how much trouble he'll be."

## Tash

Olly called Trieste on the move, following the Sheriff up and out of the station at a distance.

"I just saw Lil Sis and Malik get into some shit with the Sheriff. He took them. I know they just robbed what I think was
a Ravnos stash somewhere, so who knows what they're into. I'm following them now. He said he was taking them to Tash."

Trieste went quiet. When she spoke again it was low and measured, in a voice he had never heard out of her.

"Tash is the one who does the branding," she said. "They're not going to be branded. They're going to their final death."

"Okay."
